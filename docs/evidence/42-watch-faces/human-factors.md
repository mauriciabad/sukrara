# D — Showing data people read in a glance: science and safety-critical practice

Scope: evidence, numbers and conventions for a watch face that carries one vital value (glucose + trend) among everyday data. It contains knowledge, not a design. Where sources disagree, both sides are given. Tags: **[3rd]** = secondary or practitioner source, or a claim not checked against the primary text; **[calc]** = my own arithmetic from the cited numbers; **[inf]** = my inference, not stated by the source.

**Reference geometry used below [calc].** Mean watch-reading distance is 28 cm (SD 5) and the mean watch pitch is 50° (SD 14°, range 36–64°), measured with motion capture ([Blascheck 2019](https://petra.isenberg.cc/publications/papers/Blascheck_2018_GVS.pdf)). The owner gives the Descent Mk3i face as ~1.2″. Assuming the 43 mm model's 390 × 390 px panel (unverified here), that is 0.078 mm/px (~325 ppi). At 28 cm, 1 arcmin ≈ 0.081 mm ≈ **1.0 px**, so 16′ ≈ 17 px, 20–22′ ≈ 21–23 px and 30′ ≈ 31 px. At 33 cm (+1 SD) the same angles are about 20 / 25–27 / 37 px. The whole face subtends ~6.2° at 28 cm, a solid angle of 0.0093 sr.

## 1. The glance: how long, how often, from where

- **A time check lasts ~1.9 s.** Telling the time made up 50% of watch interactions but only 14% of usage time. A whole look at the watch averages 5–7 s. → A face competes for under 2 s of attention per look, and most of that goes to the time. [Pizza et al. 2016](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Smartwatch-in-vivo-Pizza-Brown/9508762477c897fbd687b0b29226ef73ce140dc8); figure as summarised in [Islam et al. review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.16185)
- **Half of 798,423 logged smartwatch sessions (307 users) lasted ≤5 s.** Mean session length was 7.94 s when the user started it and 10.67 s when a notification did, against ~3 min 49 s for a phone session. Caveat: 22.1% of sessions had the exact length of the hard-coded screen timeout, so the 5 s "peek" boundary is partly a platform artifact. → Measured glance lengths follow the device's timeout. They are not a pure human constant. [Visuri et al. 2017](https://ubicomp.oulu.fi/files/chi17.pdf)
- **Over 70% of activity-tracker use is glances**, meaning brief ~5 s checks with no further interaction. → The face itself is the product. Detail screens are secondary. [Gouveia et al. 2016](http://persuasive.cut.ac.cy/2016/07/22/exploring-the-design-space-of-glanceable-feedback-for-physical-activity-trackers/)
- **Viewing pose:** distance 27.6 cm sitting and 28 cm standing. The line of sight is offset 10° (SD 8°) from the face normal, so people read the watch slightly obliquely. → Legibility budgets should use ~28 cm and an oblique view, not a straight-on view at 50 cm. [Blascheck 2019](https://petra.isenberg.cc/publications/papers/Blascheck_2018_GVS.pdf)
- **Extreme angles cost brightness and contrast.** At 0° (watch flat), the OLED lost so much brightness and contrast that the researchers had to recolour the stimuli bright magenta and remove the complication backgrounds. Bar charts were not measurably slower at 0°, 22° or 50° (n = 14); radial charts showed a trend toward slower reading. [Blascheck 2023](https://petra.isenberg.cc/publications/papers/Blascheck_2023_SOP.pdf)
- **Motion degrades reading.** Walking at 2, 4 and 6 km/h lowered accuracy, and to some extent response time, when reading watch-face micro visualisations. Path shape (straight, circle, figure-eight) did not matter. Radial progress was read best at the quarters (25/50/75%), and participants asked for tick marks. → Lab thresholds are best cases. [Grioui et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17893)
- **Driving benchmarks.** NHTSA's visual-manual guideline: glances away from the road ≤2 s (85% of glances) and ≤12 s total per task. The 12 s figure comes from the 85th-percentile radio-tuning task, 11.97 s ([Federal Register 2013](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2013/04/26/2013-09883/visual-manual-nhtsa-driver-distraction-guidelines-for-in-vehicle-electronic-devices)). In naturalistic driving, eyes-off-road time totalling >2 s raised near-crash/crash risk at least twofold ([Klauer et al. 2006](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/62931/dot_62931_DS1.pdf)). In a simulator, reading a notification on a smartwatch produced longer glances (mean 1.10 s) and more glances >1.6 s than on a phone, plus slower braking ([Giang, Chen & Donmez 2017](https://hfast.mie.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/J30_Journal_Giang_Chen_Donmez_IJMHCI_2017.pdf)). → Some glances happen at the wheel. The 2 s ceiling is the established safety envelope for a single look.

## 2. What can be read in a glance: smartwatch visualisation studies

- **Thresholds for comparing two values** (~91% correct, 7/12/24 data values, 28.7 mm stimulus): donut 159–216 ms, bar 245–285 ms, radial bar 1548–1772 ms. Radial bars were roughly 6× slower. [Blascheck 2019](https://petra.isenberg.cc/publications/papers/Blascheck_2018_GVS.pdf)
- **Part-to-whole with three progress items:** bar 318 ms, radial bar 397 ms, text "70%" 503 ms. Text was slowest and least accurate (31% error against 24% for bars). Adding an analog dial as a distractor cost <55 ms. Participants rated the radial chart most beautiful and the bar most efficient. The authors frame this as a free choice between ~80 ms of speed and aesthetics. They do not recommend pure text for several proportions, and they explicitly leave single real-time values (e.g. heart rate) untested. [Blascheck 2023](https://petra.isenberg.cc/publications/papers/Blascheck_2023_SOP.pdf)
- **What faces actually carry.** In a survey of 237 wearers, the median face showed 5 data items (3–5 most common). Icon+text was the most common representation and charts were rare. Battery was the most frequent item (165 mentions), then temperature and steps ([Islam et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.00750)). A review of 358 top-selling faces found time digital 60.3%, analog 26.3% and hybrid 13.4%. 43.3% used one hue, 24.6% two and 12.6% three. 80% had no animation, and none of the animation they did use represented data. Containers were the most common decoration (254 faces) ([Islam et al. review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.16185)). The review's design space covers time display, complication count/type, decorations, hues, UI style (skeuomorphic/flat), animation and representation type. It names *personal fashion preference* as a factor, and decorations as competitors for attention.
- **Line graphs on small screens.** G-Sparks compresses line graphs along the x-axis, where Tufte's sparklines compress the y-axis, and tests slope- and height-detection tasks at a glance ([Neshati et al. 2019](https://hal.science/hal-02891743)). I did not extract the numeric results.
- **Six qualities of glanceable feedback** from 21 concepts: abstract; integrated with existing activities; supports comparison to targets/norms; actionable; builds checking habits; a proxy to further engagement. [Gouveia et al. 2016](http://persuasive.cut.ac.cy/2016/07/22/exploring-the-design-space-of-glanceable-feedback-for-physical-activity-trackers/)
- **Diabetes precedent:** simple icon/emoji warnings on a Wear OS diabetes-diary face were reported to motivate monitoring. [3rd] via the [Islam et al. review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.16185)

## 3. Perception foundations, condensed

- **Accuracy ranking of encodings** (Cleveland & McGill): position on a common scale > position on non-aligned scales > length, direction, angle > area > volume, curvature > shading/saturation. Their design rule is to use tasks as high in the ranking as possible. A crowdsourced replication confirmed the ranking ([Heer & Bostock 2010](http://vis.stanford.edu/files/2010-MTurk-CHI.pdf)). → A ring or arc encodes by angle or arc length; a bar or tape encodes by position. The smartwatch results in §2 partly diverge from this ranking (see §11). [Cleveland & McGill 1984](http://lenagroeger.s3.amazonaws.com/newschool/ClevelandMcGill.pdf)
- **Bertin's variables:** position, size, value (lightness), hue, orientation, texture, shape. Only position and size read as quantities; value is ordered; hue is non-ordered and suits categories. → Mapping a magnitude to hue, or ordered states to shape, goes against the variable's nature. [Axis Maps summary of Bertin](https://www.axismaps.com/guide/visual-variables) [3rd]
- **Preattentive processing:** tasks done in <200–250 ms regardless of the number of distractors. The features include hue, intensity, size, orientation, length, curvature, closure, density, number, flicker, and motion direction/velocity. A target defined by a *conjunction* of features is not preattentive. Interference is asymmetric: colour variation can mask shape patterns. → The 159–400 ms chart thresholds in §2 sit at the edge of preattentive time. An urgent state carried by a single unique feature can pop out; one carried by a combination of features cannot. [Healey, Perception in Visualization](https://www.csc2.ncsu.edu/faculty/healey/PP/) (companion to Healey & Enns, TVCG 2012)
- **Gestalt grouping**: proximity, similarity, common region, continuity, closure, figure/ground, common fate. → On a ~6° face, grouping alone tells the viewer which number goes with which unit or icon. [IxDF overview](https://ixdf.org/literature/topics/gestalt-principles) [3rd]
- **Sparklines** are "small, intense, simple, word-sized graphics with typographic resolution." Practices: show the normal range as a gray band; tie the last point to its number with a matching colour accent; bank slopes toward ~45°; avoid "vulgar stoplight" colour. Tufte's own worked example is a **patient's last 80 glucose readings**, with the latest value at the right edge in the colour of the printed number and a gray band for the normal range. [Tufte, Sparkline theory and practice](https://www.edwardtufte.com/notebook/sparkline-theory-and-practice-edward-tufte/)
- **Numbers need context.** "When a numeric readout is not associated with any scale, tape, or pointer, it may be difficult for pilots to determine the margin relative to targets or limits… A scale, dial, or tape may be needed" (AC 25-11B §5.7.2.2). The number of significant digits should match the data's accuracy, with no leading zeros (§5.7.2.1). Graphics belong only when they add information or cut interpretation time or error (§5.7.4.1). [FAA AC 25-11B](https://www.faa.gov/documentlibrary/media/advisory_circular/ac_25-11b.pdf)

## 4. Numbers and type

- **Character height standards.** ISO 9241-303: minimum 16′ of arc, with 20–22′ required to be achievable (via the DIN EN text). The FAA HFDS gives the same 16′ minimum and 20–22′ preferred, and 10′ for non-critical, non-time-critical information. MIL-STD-1472F gives 16′ minimum and 20′ preferred for monochrome; a vendor summary says colour displays need 21′ minimum and 30′ preferred [3rd]. Heights are cap height ("H"). → At 28 cm these are ≈17 / 21–23 / 10 / 31 px on the Mk3i [calc]. Note: these standards assume steady reading, not 300 ms glances. [ISO/HFDS summary](https://github.com/danielrosehill/Claude-Monitor-Research/blob/main/docs/readability-geometry.md) [3rd]; [UXmatters on ISO 9241-3](https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2009/01/text-treatment-and-the-user-interface.php) [3rd]; [MIL-STD-1472F](https://www.denix.osd.mil/soh/denix-files/sites/21/2016/03/02_MIL-STD-1472F-Human-Engineering.pdf); [LCD vendor summary](http://www.liquidcrystaltechnologies.com/tech_support/CharacterHeight.htm) [3rd]
- **Typeface genre changes glance time.** In a driving simulator, a humanist face (Frutiger) cut men's total glance time by 10.6% (0.43 s per task) and errors by 3.1% against a square grotesque (Eurostile, the "technical/sporty" look). The effect was weaker or absent for women. [Reimer et al. 2014](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/96509)
- **Case and width.** Lowercase needed 26% more time to read accurately at a glance, and condensed text 11.2% more. Uppercase is more legible, partly because it is bigger, but it brings more letter confusions (Bouma). [Sawyer et al. 2017, "The Cost of Cool"](https://www.bendsawyer.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Sawyer_et-al_2017_The-Cost-of-Cool-Typographic-Style-Legibility-in-Reading.pdf)
- **Size, crowding and spacing.** Smaller text (3 vs 4 mm at their distance) needed 26.7% more glance time, and tight leading 8.9% more. Wider leading did not compensate for small size. Crowded displays read slower than isolated words. Lighter weights with more negative space may read better than heavy ones (Dobres et al. 2016b, cited there). [Dobres et al. 2018](https://benwolfe.net/pdf/Dobres_AppErgo_2018.pdf)
- **Polarity × ambient light.** In bright surroundings, dark-on-light and light-on-dark text are almost equally legible. In dark surroundings, light-on-dark thresholds rise sharply ([Dobres, Chahine & Reimer 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28166901/)). The positive-polarity advantage grows as characters get smaller ([Piepenbrock et al. 2014](https://www.psychologie.hhu.de/fileadmin/redaktion/Oeffentliche_Medien/Fakultaeten/Mathematisch-Naturwissenschaftliche_Fakultaet/Psychologie/AAP/Publikationen/in_press/Piepenbrock_Mayr_Buchner_inpress_.pdf)), and the proposed mechanism is pupil constriction ([Piepenbrock, pupil size](https://www.psychologie.hhu.de/fileadmin/redaktion/Oeffentliche_Medien/Fakultaeten/Mathematisch-Naturwissenschaftliche_Fakultaet/Psychologie/AAP/Publikationen/in_press/Piepenbrock-in_press-Smaller_pupil_size_and_better.pdf)). → This bites at night on a dark OLED face (see §11).
- **Digit confusions.** Segmented numerals that differ by few segments are confused most (van Nes & Bouma 1980). A redesigned seven-segment font cut errors by ~50% ([Gunderson et al. 1991](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/legibility-of-seven-segment-numeric-led-displays-comparisons-of-t/)). Dot-matrix and segment digits can be improved by design ([Visible Language 1976](https://journals.uc.edu/index.php/vl/article/view/5201)). Segmented decimals invite out-by-ten errors ([Thimbleby, "Reasons to question seven segment displays"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/266653791_Reasons_to_question_seven_segment_displays)) [3rd]. → A glucose value with a decimal (mmol/L) or a 1/7, 3/8 or 5/6 pair is the error surface.
- **Rolling digits** must not create "confusing motion effects such that the apparent motion does not match the actual trend." [AC 25-11B §5.7.2.1](https://www.faa.gov/documentlibrary/media/advisory_circular/ac_25-11b.pdf)
- **Historic lesson.** The three-pointer altimeter took ~7 s to read and produced >11% errors of ≥1,000 ft (Grether 1949). The fix was a counter plus a pointer. [Sussex summary](http://users.sussex.ac.uk/~blayw/flyingl.html) [3rd]
- **Tabular figures.** With proportional digits, a changing number shifts its own width on every update (11:11 is narrower than 18:18). Practice is fixed-width figures for clocks and live values. [Practitioner note](https://seangeng.com/writing/tabular-nums-for-changing-numbers) [3rd]

## 5. Colour and light

- **Colour-vision deficiency** affects about 1 in 12 men ([NEI](https://www.nei.nih.gov/eye-health-information/eye-conditions-and-diseases/color-blindness)) and 1 in 200 women, mostly red/green ([Colour Blind Awareness](https://www.colourblindawareness.org/colour-blindness/)) [3rd for women]. The owner is male.
- **Never colour alone.** "If color is used for coding, at least one other distinctive coding parameter should be used (size, shape, location…)". Normal ageing reduces the eye's ability to focus red and to tell blue from green (§5.8.1). Use ≤6 coding colours (§5.8.3). Don't make distinctions with shades of one hue (§5.8.9). Pure blue has low luminance and should not carry important information (§5.8.9). A symbol must be large enough for its colour to be read (§5.8.10). Pairs to avoid (§5.8.7): saturated red/blue, red/green, blue/green, yellow/green; yellow on white; magenta on green; magenta on black; blue on black; red on black. [FAA AC 25-11B](https://www.faa.gov/documentlibrary/media/advisory_circular/ac_25-11b.pdf)
- **Aviation colour meanings:** red = warning, amber/yellow = caution, green = engaged/normal, white = scales and current values, cyan = sky/armed modes, magenta = "fly-to" targets (Table 5-1). 14 CFR 25.1322 makes red = warning and amber = caution mandatory, and advisories may be neither red nor green. → These meanings are learned conventions that the owner carries from CGMs, cars and the Descent itself. [AC 25-11B](https://www.faa.gov/documentlibrary/media/advisory_circular/ac_25-11b.pdf); [25.1322 final rule](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/2022-11/Final%20Rule%2014%20CFR%20part%2025.1322,%20Flightcrew%20Alerting.pdf)
- **Contrast floors.** WCAG 1.4.11 asks for ≥3:1 between graphical objects needed to understand content and adjacent colours ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)). ISO 15008 (in-vehicle) sets minimum symbol/background contrast of 5:1 at night, 3:1 at twilight and in daylight, and 2:1 in direct sun, and allows outlines when contrast fails at dawn and dusk ([ISO 15008:2017 summary](https://www.normsplash.com/FreeDownload/162153284/DIN-EN-ISO-15008-2017-en.PDF)) [3rd].
- **OLED in sun and dark.** Ambient contrast ratio = (L_on + reflected) / (L_off + reflected). OLED has the highest ACR in the dark, but it "declines sharply as ambient light gets brighter" ([Chen et al., Optics Express 2017](https://opg.optica.org/oe/fulltext.cfm?uri=oe-25-26-33643&id=380371); [Chen et al., LSA 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/lsa2017168)). Transflective MIP gains contrast in sunlight while AMOLED has to raise its luminance ([practitioner comparison](https://gadgetsandwearables.com/2023/05/17/amoled-vs-mip-screen/)) [3rd]. → A palette that works indoors can wash out at the beach or on a boat.
- **Garmin AMOLED always-on**: updates once a minute, ≤10% of pixels lit (v2). The v1 burn-in guard also kept any pixel from staying lit >4 min. A gesture lifts the limits for a short per-second period ([Garmin CIQ Entry Points](https://developer.garmin.com/connect-iq/articles/user-experience-guidelines/Entry_Points.html)). Forum reports say CIQ 5 replaced the pixel count with a 10% *luminance* budget [3rd, [Garmin forum](https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/f/discussion/322419/always-on-display-sleep-mode-behaviour-and-rules-on-newer-amoled-watches)]. → The resting face is physically a sparse, dim display.
- **Night and dark adaptation.** Full dark adaptation needs ≥30 min of darkness, and dim red light gives moderate adaptation in ~20 min. Red light "severely distorts colors" and washes out red, so it is advisable only when outside night vision matters ([FAA AIM 8-1-6 via search](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/atpubs/aim_html/chap8_section_1.html)). Bright light for >1 s impairs night vision ([FAA Pilot Vision brochure](https://www.faa.gov/pilots/safety/pilotsafetybrochures/media/pilot_vision.pdf)). Displays should dim to levels that preserve dark adaptation ([AC 25-11B §3.2.3.1](https://www.faa.gov/documentlibrary/media/advisory_circular/ac_25-11b.pdf)). The Descent Mk3 has a **Red Shift** mode (shades of red, green or orange) for low light ([Garmin manual](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/GUID-9183E86B-2399-4CFC-AB50-EAFC6D6ED326/EN-US/Descent_Mk3_Series_OM_EN-US.pdf)). → In a red-shifted view, red-coded states lose their hue meaning [inf].
- **Underwater** (this watch dives). Water absorbs red first, then orange and yellow. Reported depths disagree: red is gone by ~5 m ([DivePhotoGuide](https://www.divephotoguide.com/getting-started-with-underwater-photography/basic-principles-light-underwater/)) or by ~6 m ([ScubaDiverInfo](https://www.scubadiverinfo.com/2_red_at_depth.html)) [3rd]. This applies to *reflected* light over the path length. An emissive screen 30 cm from the mask is probably much less affected, but no study was found [inf].

## 6. Motion, change and flashing

- **Change blindness:** large changes go unnoticed across blinks, saccades or blank screens. → Each glance at a screen that was off or dim is a "flicker paradigm": the viewer has no memory of the previous frame to compare against, so a change between glances is invisible unless the display itself encodes "changed" [inf]. [Simons & Rensink 2005](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~rensink/publications/download/S&R-TICS-05a.pdf)
- **Motion is the strongest peripheral signal.** Peripheral colour targets went undetected 25% of the time (6% near the centre), while motion stayed under 2% at both distances. Shape detection slowed from 2.0 to 4.4 s in the periphery; motion did not slow. Their guidelines: motion does not interfere with colour/shape coding; small periodic motions signal well; slow linear oscillation is a good all-round signal; motion "can contribute strongly to distraction and irritation." [Bartram, Ware & Calvert 2003](https://interruptions.net/literature/Bartram-IJHCS03-BW.pdf)
- **Motion of data in aviation.** Moving symbols must not blur, shimmer, jitter or ratchet. Smoothing must not add significant error or lag (§5.9.1). A symbol at the limit of its range should slide from view, change appearance, or make the limit self-evident (§5.9.2). Trend indicators "should have appropriate hysteresis and damping to be useful and non-distracting; however, damping may result in erroneous airspeed when accelerating" (A.4.5). [AC 25-11B](https://www.faa.gov/documentlibrary/media/advisory_circular/ac_25-11b.pdf)
- **Blinking.** Use 0.8–4 Hz. If blinking can last more than ~10 s, provide a way to cancel it (AC 25-11B §5.11.4). IEC 60601-1-8 discourages flashing text because it is hard to read, and allows alternating normal and reverse video instead ([IEC 60601-1-8 §6.3.2 text](https://standards.har-el.com/Projects/181701/60601-1-8/html/6-3-2-Visual-Generation.htm)).
- **Photosensitivity:** no more than 3 general or red flashes per second, or the flashing area must stay under 0.006 sr in any 10° field ([WCAG 2.3.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html)). → The whole face at 28 cm is 0.0093 sr (0.0067 sr at 33 cm), so a full-face flash fails the area test. Only up to ~65% of the face can flash [calc].
- **Reduced motion:** animation triggered by interaction should be possible to turn off. Vestibular disorders cause dizziness, nausea and headaches from non-essential motion. [WCAG 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)

## 7. Calm and peripheral displays

- **Calm technology** "engages both the center and the periphery of our attention, and in fact moves back and forth between the two." Periphery is "what we are attuned to without attending to explicitly." [Weiser & Brown 1995](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rudolph/Teaching/weiser.pdf)
- **Case's principles:** require the smallest possible amount of attention; inform and create calm; use the periphery; communicate without speaking; **work even when it fails** ("does it default to a usable state?"); use the minimum technology; **respect social norms**. [calmtech.com](https://calmtech.com/)
- **Ambient-display heuristics** (adapted from Nielsen): useful and relevant information; "sufficient information design" ("just enough"); consistent and intuitive mapping (minimal cognitive load, recognition over recall); **visibility of state**, with easily noticeable transitions; easy transition to more detail; "peripherality" (unobtrusive until attention is needed); aesthetic match with the environment ("notice an ambient display because of a change in the data… not because its design clashes"). In their field test, people thought one display "was broken." [Mankoff et al. 2003](https://faculty.washington.edu/garyhs/docs/mankoff-CHI2003-heuristics.pdf)
- **Taxonomy dimensions:** information capacity, notification level, representational fidelity (direct ↔ abstract, in semiotic terms) and aesthetic emphasis. Notification levels run user poll → change blind → make aware → interrupt → demand attention. Ambient systems by definition show "important but **not critical**" information, with subtle changes. → A glucose face spans the whole scale, from ambient at normal values to demand-attention at a low. [Pousman & Stasko 2006](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~stasko/papers/avi06.pdf)
- **Dark cockpit.** Airbus overhead panels are lit only "to indicate where an action is required": "if all the lights are out, then the aircraft is ready to fly." Caveat: dark ≠ verified OK, so pilots cross-check the system page. [Airbus A330neo cockpit](https://www.airbus.com/sites/g/files/jlcbta136/files/2021-10/A330neo-cockpit-commonality-with-A350-innovations.pdf); [caveat, 3rd](https://flyawaysimulation.com/ask/answers/airbus-a320-overhead-panel-how-to-use/)

## 8. Alarms and alarm fatigue

- **72–99% of clinical alarms are false or need no action.** The result is desensitisation and missed alarms; deaths have been attributed to it. Customising thresholds is one of the measures that reduced false alarms. [Sendelbach & Funk 2013](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Alarm-fatigue:-a-patient-safety-concern.-Sendelbach-Funk/f799e0bf338f78e757ee42d922ba2a57ab2ed7d8)
- **Diabetes devices:** alarm fatigue constrains safe use. Its two frames are "patient safety first" and human factors ([Shivers et al. 2013](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/237199114_Turn_It_Off_Diabetes_Device_Alarm_Fatigue_Considerations_for_the_Present_and_the_Future)). Unnecessary, erroneous or too-frequent alarms lead people to ignore them or switch them off, including at night to protect their own or a partner's sleep ([Roberts, Walsh & Heinemann 2014](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/1932296814532882)). A 2025 review calls alarm fatigue the most common reason for stopping diabetes technology [3rd, [Children review](https://doi.org/10.3390/children12121668)].
- **Medical alarm encoding (IEC 60601-1-8).** High priority: red, flashing 1.4–2.8 Hz. Medium: yellow, 0.4–0.8 Hz. Low: cyan or yellow, steady. Duty cycle 20–60%. Bursts are 10/3/1–2 pulses. Optional marks are "!", "!!", "!!!". Priority must be perceivable at 4 m, and the specific condition legible at 1 m. Wearable systems do not need the indicator light. Information signals must look different from alarm signals ([IEC clause text](https://standards.har-el.com/Projects/181701/60601-1-8/html/6-3-2-Visual-Generation.htm); [table via JBTH 2023](https://jbth.com.br/index.php/JBTH/article/download/322/259/); [patent quoting rates](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11797158)) [3rd for Table 2 values]. → Priority is coded redundantly: colour, flash rate, sound pattern and glyph count.
- **Persistence before alerting.** The Descent's "ascending too fast" alert fires only after >9.1 m/min **for more than 5 s**. [Garmin Mk3 manual](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/GUID-9183E86B-2399-4CFC-AB50-EAFC6D6ED326/EN-US/Descent_Mk3_Series_OM_EN-US.pdf)

## 9. Privacy and social perception

- **Shoulder surfing is opportunistic, not malicious.** Its motives are curiosity (33.6%), boredom (33.6%), "device in line of sight" and "reaction to stimulus." What gets read is mostly text (47%), and it is personal in 84% of incidents. 21.8% of participants said it happens regularly. Users responded by switching to harmless apps or looking up to check for observers. → A changing or bright element is itself a stimulus that draws bystanders' eyes [inf from "reaction to stimulus"]. Phones only: no smartwatch-specific shoulder-surfing study was found. [Eiband et al. 2017](https://www.medien.ifi.lmu.de/pubdb/publications/pub/eiband2017chi/eiband2017chi.pdf)
- **Obfuscation precedents:** handwritten-style fonts deter casual readers ([My Scrawl Hides It All, CHI EA 2016](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/302073980_My_Scrawl_Hides_It_All_Protecting_Text_Messages_Against_Shoulder_Surfing_With_Handwritten_Fonts)), and gaze-contingent reveal hides the rest of the screen ([EyeSpot 2018](https://dx.doi.org/10.3390/mti2030045)).
- **Visibility of diabetes devices cuts both ways.** Some people, especially adolescents, conceal devices or avoid using them in public ([PLOS ONE 2025](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0352590); [IJQHC review](https://academic.oup.com/intqhc/article/37/3/mzaf046/8127067)). Others treat visibility as identity and "quiet defiance" ([ultra-endurance T1D study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13154725/)). CGM can also *reduce* the visibility of diabetes at work compared with finger-sticks ([Scharf et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1177/1932296818789143)). Early CGM users described the device as both "best friend" and "spy" ([meta-synthesis](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/321862509_Best_friend_or_spy_A_qualitative_meta-synthesis_on_the_impact_of_continuous_glucose_monitoring_on_life_with_Type_1_diabetes)).
- **The "AT effect."** Observers judged head-mounted display use more socially acceptable when it supported a person with a disability ([Profita et al. 2016](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/2858036.2858130)) [3rd for the exact wording]. Similarly, a head-worn device labelled "medical" was rated more acceptable than the same device labelled "brain fitness tool" (d = 0.78, N = 1,387), and bulk lowered acceptance ([Kelly & Gilbert 2018](https://doi.org/10.1177/1541931218621237)). → Being read as medical is not automatically stigmatising. The stigma literature and the acceptability literature point in different directions (see §11).
- **Fashion and social acceptability are separate constructs** (WEAR-scale item analysis) ([Ouverson, Kelly & Gilbert 2017](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-58071-5_16)). Watches must be "a traditional watch" *and* allow "personal expression" ([Visuri et al. 2017](https://ubicomp.oulu.fi/files/chi17.pdf)). The face design space includes personal fashion preference ([Islam et al. review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.16185)). 62.4% never change their face ([Islam et al. review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.16185)). → A face is often chosen once and lived with.
- **Abandonment:** a third of US activity-tracker owners stopped within 6 months, and more than half eventually stopped ([Endeavour Partners 2014](http://pop.h-cdn.co/assets/cm/15/06/54d15565175a0_-_Endeavour-Partners-Wearables-White-Paper-20141.pdf)). Abandonment is studied from resale ads in [Clawson et al. 2015](https://jamesclawson.com/publications/) [3rd: reasons not extracted].

## 10. Analogous safety-critical displays: what transfers

**Aviation primary flight displays**
- **Speed trend vector:** an arrow from the current speed to the speed predicted **10 s ahead** at the current acceleration. It is green on Boeing and yellow on Airbus ([Boeing description via PPRuNe](https://www.pprune.org/tech-log/102147-737ng-airspeed-trend-vector.html); [A320 PFD](https://flyawaysimulation.com/ask/answers/airbus-a320-pfd-how-to-read/)) [3rd]. It appears only above a ~4.5 kt trend ([EASA training module](https://klmukiaa.com/mod/page/view.php?id=3967)) [3rd]. On Gulfstreams, the speed digits turn amber when the trend vector *reaches* the low-speed band, so the alert is predictive ([FAA low-airspeed report](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/2022-11/ASHWG%20LAA%20Report_15%20March%20Final%20Version_Appendix%201.pdf)). Higher numbers sit at the top, so increasing energy moves the cue upward ([AC 25-11B A.4.5](https://www.faa.gov/documentlibrary/media/advisory_circular/ac_25-11b.pdf)). → Transfers: value + fixed-horizon projection, a dead band, damping/hysteresis, and an "up = more" spatial mapping.
- **Bad data must not be usable.** Lost or erroneous primary data is removed or crossed out with an "X" (§4.6.4). The detector of the failure must be independent of its source (§4.6.5). [AC 25-11B](https://www.faa.gov/documentlibrary/media/advisory_circular/ac_25-11b.pdf) → Stale glucose is the watch's "failed sensor."

**Cars**
- 2 s / 12 s glance limits, and the ≥2× risk above 2 s (§1). ISO 15008 contrast by lighting condition (§5). The humanist-typeface effect (§4).

**Medical monitors**
- IEC 60601-1-8 priority coding, perceptibility at 4 m and 1 m, and intelligent suppression of lower-priority alarms (§8). Alarm fatigue (§8).

**Dive computers** (the owner's own instrument)
- **Shearwater:** yellow = "not immediately dangerous but should be addressed." Flashing red = "could be life threatening if not immediately addressed." The level is readable **without colour**: warnings are solid inverted, and critical alerts flash between inverted and normal ([Perdix manual](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/1365375/Shearwater-Perdix.html)). Ascent rate is shown as arrows, 1 arrow per 3 m/min: white at 1–3 arrows, yellow at 4–5, flashing red at 6 (>18 m/min). NDL turns yellow below 5 min ([Teric manual](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0838/3732/1530/files/Teric_Manual_RevH5.pdf?v=1731106441)).
- **Garmin Descent Mk3:** the ascent-rate bar is green <7.9 m/min, yellow 7.9–10.1 and red >10.1. The tissue-load bar is green 0–79%, yellow 80–99% and red ≥100%. There is a "Big Numbers" layout and the Red Shift mode ([Garmin manual](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/GUID-9183E86B-2399-4CFC-AB50-EAFC6D6ED326/EN-US/Descent_Mk3_Series_OM_EN-US.pdf)). → The owner already reads one vital rate as a coloured bar on this exact screen.
- **ISO 6425 dive watches:** time must be readable at **25 cm in total darkness**, minute marks must be distinguishable from hour marks, and a **"running" indicator** must show at a glance that the watch works ([Webchronos on ISO 6425:2018](https://en.webchronos.net/features/111563/); [divewatch.com](https://divewatch.com/reference/iso-6425/)) [3rd; test durations conflict]. → A liveness cue is part of the standard.

**Sports watches**
- Garmin's default exercise screens use large black digits on white with no visualisations. The Islam review questions whether this is the most effective choice under divided attention ([Islam et al. review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.16185)) [3rd].
- Motorsport dashes, cycling computers: not researched in this pass (search budget exhausted).

## 11. Contradictions and open questions

1. **Text vs graphics for one value.** Text proportions were slowest (503 ms vs 318 ms; [Blascheck 2023](https://petra.isenberg.cc/publications/papers/Blascheck_2023_SOP.pdf)). But those tasks were proportion judgements, and single live values like glucose are explicitly untested. Meanwhile, text is what wearers actually use most ([Islam 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.00750)). Aviation pairs readouts with tapes because a number alone hides the margin ([AC 25-11B](https://www.faa.gov/documentlibrary/media/advisory_circular/ac_25-11b.pdf)).
2. **Radial vs linear.** Donuts beat bars in 2019, but bars beat radial bars in 2023, and radial bars were ~6× slower in 2019. The ring is perceived as more beautiful (2023). Cleveland & McGill rank angle below position. Results depend on the task and the variant.
3. **Polarity at night on OLED.** Legibility research favours dark-on-light, especially for small text in dark surroundings ([Dobres 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28166901/); [Piepenbrock 2014](https://www.psychologie.hhu.de/fileadmin/redaktion/Oeffentliche_Medien/Fakultaeten/Mathematisch-Naturwissenschaftliche_Fakultaet/Psychologie/AAP/Publikationen/in_press/Piepenbrock_Mayr_Buchner_inpress_.pdf)). Dark adaptation, Garmin's ≤10% always-on budget, burn-in and battery all push toward sparse light-on-dark. Unresolved for a 1.2″ emissive screen.
4. **Stroke weight.** Lighter weights may read better ([Dobres 2018](https://benwolfe.net/pdf/Dobres_AppErgo_2018.pdf)), and Garmin advises thin fonts in low power. But the 16′ minimum assumes adequate contrast, and thin strokes lose contrast first in sunlight and at oblique angles [inf]. No study tested emissive digits at watch size.
5. **Uppercase:** more legible at a glance, but more letter confusions ([Sawyer 2017](https://www.bendsawyer.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Sawyer_et-al_2017_The-Cost-of-Cool-Typographic-Style-Legibility-in-Reading.pdf)).
6. **Red/green convention vs CVD.** Aviation, Garmin Descent and Shearwater all use green/yellow/red. ~8% of men cannot rely on red-green contrast, and the FAA itself lists saturated red-green as a pair to avoid. Shearwater's inverted and flashing redundancy is one documented answer. Red Shift and dim red night lighting erase the red meaning.
7. **Visible medical identity.** Stigma studies report concealment, while acceptability studies (AT effect, "medical" label d = 0.78) report *higher* acceptance when a device reads as medical. Neither tested watch faces. The owner's own goal ("doesn't scream diabetes") is a personal preference that these studies neither confirm nor refute.
8. **Subtle change vs change blindness.** Ambient theory asks for subtle changes ([Pousman & Stasko](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~stasko/papers/avi06.pdf)), but between-glance changes are invisible without encoding ([Simons & Rensink](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~rensink/publications/download/S&R-TICS-05a.pdf)). When the wrist is down, the screen is out of view, so peripheral-motion research mostly applies to the moment the wrist is raised [inf].
9. **Flash rules disagree.** The ranges are 1.4–2.8 Hz (IEC high), 0.8–4 Hz with cancellation after ~10 s (FAA), and ≤3 flashes/s plus an area limit (WCAG). A full-face flash at wrist distance exceeds the WCAG area [calc].
10. **Trend damping:** less jitter versus lag/error ([AC 25-11B A.4.5](https://www.faa.gov/documentlibrary/media/advisory_circular/ac_25-11b.pdf)). CGM data is already smoothed by the sensor, and the right amount of extra damping for a 1-minute series is unknown.
11. **External validity.** Thresholds come from seated or standing lab tasks at a fixed 28–50° angle. Walking reduces accuracy, the flat-wrist angle loses contrast, and the "5 s glance" is partly a screen-timeout artifact.
12. **Gaps not covered here:** round-display layout research, motorsport and cycling dashboards, and primary text for ISO 15008 character height, MIL-STD-1472 colour figures and IEC 60601-1-8 Table 2. Web search was exhausted, so these are unverified.
