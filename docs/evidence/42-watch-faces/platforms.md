# A. What the smartwatch platforms say about watch faces (Apple, Wear OS, Samsung, Pebble, Fitbit, Zepp, Huawei; not Garmin)

Scope: official guidelines, developer docs, store rules, talks and designer interviews, read November 2026. Each bullet gives the finding → why it matters for a face → source. "(old)" marks guidance from before 2018. "(3rd-party)" and "(unverified)" mark claims that don't come from the platform itself. Units differ between platforms (pt, dp/sp, px), so numbers can't be compared one to one.

## 1. What a face is for: purpose, glance time, hierarchy

- **Wear OS states three principles: time-telling, expressive, useful.** "People check the time 150 times a day. Ensure that time is highly visible." Faces should "facilitate customization" and use complications for the rest. → On Wear OS, time comes first and data is secondary. [Wear OS: Watch faces](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/wear/guides/surfaces/watch-faces)
- **Apple measures interactions in seconds.** "Our internal metric for interaction times is about five seconds or less" (WWDC15). The Smart Stack talk (WWDC23) gives "around ten seconds max" per widget glance. → This is a time budget for any data a face shows. [WWDC15 Designing for Apple Watch](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2015/802/), [WWDC23 Smart Stack](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10309/)
- **The original Android Wear guide (old, 2014–15) asks for one message, expressed through time.** "Glanceability is the single most important principle." "A watch dial is a timeline; add data to it": instead of overlaying a temperature, show "how the temperature will change over the course of the day." "Stick to one message": show only the next event, not the whole month. "Your watch face can show users contextually relevant data and react to it by changing styles and colors." It also says to test a paper sketch by asking a friend what it means. → This is the clearest platform statement that data can be expressed through the face itself, not only through slots. [Android Wear watch faces design doc (AOSP source)](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/refs/heads/lollipop-mr1-release/docs/html/design/wear/watchfaces.jd)
- **Apple says complications should be live, not launchers.** "The complication behavior they appreciate more is the display of relevant information that always feels up to date. A static complication… may be less likely to remain in a prominent position." Each complication should deep-link to its own place in the app. → A data slot earns its place by staying current. [HIG Complications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/complications)
- **Apple's hierarchy example uses contrast, not size alone.** The Heart Rate rectangular complication draws "high-contrast white and red for the primary content and a lower-contrast gray for the graph lines and labels." → Value and context are separated by contrast. [HIG Complications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/complications)
- **Zepp publishes a default order of importance.** After the time: "steps > date > weather > battery > heart rate > others" (not mandatory). [Zepp Watchface Specification](https://docs.zepp.com/docs/watchface/specification/)
- **Pebble:** "the layout design should make it immediately obvious which part of the UI contains the vital information"; keep information density low. [Pebble Design and Interaction](https://developer.rebble.io/guides/design-and-interaction/), [Recommended Guidelines](https://developer.rebble.io/guides/design-and-interaction/recommended/)
- **Fitbit store categories show the archetypes reviewers check:** "Analog" (hands or elements that move like an analog clock), "Digital" ("clearly the hour and minute in digits") and "Stats Heavy" ("heavily feature the core fitness statistics"). A wrong tag is a reason for rejection. [Fitbit Publishing guide](https://dev.fitbit.com/build/guides/publishing/)

## 2. Round screens: layout and safe areas

- **Apple: black backgrounds merge with the bezel.** "The black color of the Watch bezel was carefully chosen to match the color of the display… If you design your app to have a black background, the bezel will provide visual padding." (old) → On OLED, a black ground makes the screen's edge disappear. [WWDC15](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2015/802/)
- **Apple's curved slots:** text in a circular complication can curve along the bezel and "fill nearly 180 degrees of the bezel before truncating." Corner slots use the screen's corners. Modular Ultra uses "the outermost edge to present real-time data, including seconds, altitude, or depth." → The edge can carry a reading as well as decoration. [HIG Complications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/complications), [Apple Watch Ultra 2 newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2023/09/apple-unveils-apple-watch-ultra-2/)
- **Wear OS:** "Stay within the bezel… minimize essential elements being cropped." Design for the smallest screen (192 dp) first, with 225 dp as the breakpoint, and "define outer margins as percentages rather than absolute values." The app quality rule WO-V16 says content must not be cut off and must fit at least a 192 dp circle. [Watch faces](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/wear/guides/surfaces/watch-faces), [Screen sizes](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/wear/guides/foundations/screen-sizes), [Wear app quality](https://developer.android.com/docs/quality-guidelines/wear-app-quality)
- **Burn-in shifting moves the whole picture.** Wear OS: when `isBurnInProtectionRequired`, the system shifts content; "Don't manually shift UI elements… place critical UI elements at least 10 pixels away from the edge." Samsung: "the face shifts a few pixels periodically, so edge elements can be pushed off-screen." → In always-on, the outermost ring is not safe for essential data. [Wear OS Always-on](https://developer.android.com/training/wearables/always-on), [Samsung Watch Face States](https://developer.samsung.com/one-ui-watch-tizen/watch-face/states.html)
- **Pebble round (Time Round 180×180; Round 2 260×260, 64-colour memory-in-pixel LCD with touch):** "requires a small two pixel border on each edge"; "Avoid thin rings around the edge… use thick rings, or inset them significantly." On a circle "each row of text can have a different width," so use paging instead of continuous reflow. "Be open to creating a new UI" for round screens instead of porting a rectangular one. [Round App Design](https://developer.rebble.io/guides/design-and-interaction/in-the-round/), [Round App UI](https://developer.repebble.com/guides/user-interfaces/round-app-ui/), [Pebble Round 2 board](https://developer.repebble.com/pebbleos/docs/boards/getafix/)
- **Zepp:** "Leave a 2px safe distance on the edge." Reserve the top and bottom for status dots and offline voice, so nothing important goes there. [Zepp customization: watchface](https://docs.zepp.com/docs/designs/customization/watchface/), [Safe area](https://docs.zepp.com/docs/designs/specifications/safe-area/)
- **System overlays take part of the face.** Pebble's Timeline Quick View covers the bottom (51 px including a 2 px border on rectangular models; compute it at runtime). Faces should lay out to `layer_get_unobstructed_bounds()`, hide non-essential layers (the example hides the date) and keep the background full-screen. Android Wear (old) placed status indicators and the notification "peek card" over faces, and faces were told to keep the time clear of them. → A face needs a compact state for when part of it is covered. [Unobstructed Area](https://developer.rebble.io/guides/user-interfaces/unobstructed-area/), [Android Wear doc](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/refs/heads/lollipop-mr1-release/docs/html/design/wear/watchfaces.jd)
- **Samsung on round composition:** on analog faces complications go "anywhere on the screen except for the center"; items along the curved edge "can express flow or indicate that the items are independent… with equal priority"; motion outward from the centre reads as "expansion or creation". [Samsung Complications](https://developer.samsung.com/one-ui-watch-tizen/watch-face/complications.html), [Circular UX](https://developer.samsung.com/one-ui-watch-tizen/circular-ux.html)

## 3. Typography and minimum sizes

- **Apple watchOS:** default 16 pt, minimum 12 pt. "Avoid Ultralight, Thin, and Light"; with a thin custom font, "aim for larger than the recommended sizes." The system font is SF Compact; "In complications, watchOS uses SF Compact Rounded." [HIG Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)
- **Apple's default complication text sizes (40 mm → 45/49 mm):** circular 12–14.5 pt Rounded Medium; corner 10–12 pt Rounded Semibold; rectangular 16.5–19.5 pt; X-Large circular 34.5–41 pt. Line widths should be "two points or greater… especially when the wearer is in motion." Corner text sits below the 12 pt app minimum. [HIG Complications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/complications)
- **Apple Dynamic Type on watchOS** runs from xSmall (Body 14 pt) to xxxLarge (Body 19 pt), plus accessibility sizes. Apps should support at least 140 % enlargement on watchOS (200 % elsewhere). [HIG Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography), [HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- **Apple's face typography is custom and offered as a choice.** Typograph offers three custom fonts (Classic, Modern, Rounded) in four scripts. Numerals Duo/Mono use "an Apple-designed font," and the San Francisco counters "reference the shape of the Watch itself." [Apple Support: faces and features](https://support.apple.com/guide/watch/faces-and-features-apde9218b440/watchos), [Wired 2015](https://www.wired.com/2015/04/apple-watch-design/)
- **Wear OS:** WO-V14 sets a minimum of 12 sp for essential text and 10 sp for non-essential text. Material 3 Expressive adds Arc and Numerals type roles and variable-font axes (weight, width) for "expressive motion feedback". [Wear app quality](https://developer.android.com/docs/quality-guidelines/wear-app-quality), [Wear typography](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/wear/guides/styles/typography)
- **Pebble:** "Consider font size 28 for larger items, and a minimum of 18 for smaller ones" (system Gothic sizes at about 176–202 dpi). Faces can read the user's Text Size setting through `preferred_content_size()`; on Pebble Time 2 and Round 2 the scale starts one step larger. [Recommended](https://developer.rebble.io/guides/design-and-interaction/recommended/), [Content Size](https://developer.repebble.com/guides/user-interfaces/content-size/)
- **Zepp:** "The minimum font size is 22px and the line thickness of the dial elements must be ≥1px." Most rules are fixed digit widths ("00000" for steps, "000" for heart rate), so layouts are sized for the widest value. [Zepp customization: watchface](https://docs.zepp.com/docs/designs/customization/watchface/)
- **Samsung (Tizen-era):** "Keep to one font throughout"; "Avoid drastic changes in font size"; use condensed widths for space. [Samsung Typography](https://developer.samsung.com/one-ui-watch-tizen/visual/typography.html)
- **Pebble, Core Devices and TTMM ship subsetted fonts.** TTMM's custom fonts held "sometimes as few as ten characters"; Wear OS Watch Face Push recommends `pyftsubset` to keep only `HH:MM` glyphs. → Display-only numeral fonts are cheap. [TTMM archive](https://ttmm.is/pebble-watch/), [Watch Face Push](https://developer.android.com/training/wearables/watch-face-push)

## 4. Colour and contrast

- **Apple contrast:** 4.5:1 up to 17 pt, 3:1 at 18 pt or bold (WCAG AA). "Convey information with more than color alone… red-green and blue-orange" are hard to tell apart. [HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- **Apple tinted ("accented") mode.** People can have a face desaturate every complication and tint it one colour. "Avoid using color as the only way to communicate important information… people get the same information in tinted mode." Full-colour, accented and vibrant are the three rendering modes, and `.widgetAccentable()` chooses which parts take the accent. → A colour-coded reading must also work in grey. [HIG Complications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/complications), [WWDC22 Complications and widgets: Reloaded](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10050/)
- **Apple on background colour:** use it "when you have something to communicate"; "Avoid using full-screen background color in views that are likely to remain onscreen for long periods." Smart Stack widgets are encouraged to use meaningful backgrounds ("Stocks uses a red background for falling… green… if a stock's value rises"). The "Large text" widget layout is suggested for "a keyword such as 'high' or 'low'". [HIG Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color), [HIG Widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets), [WWDC23](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10309/)
- **Wear OS:** "Use black as the primary color… conserve battery." "Build from black": watch UIs must work day and night. Red means error and green success. In Material 3 Expressive the system colour is derived from the user's watch face and applied to tiles. → The face sets the colour of the rest of the watch. [Watch faces](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/wear/guides/surfaces/watch-faces), [Wear Color](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/wear/guides/styles/color), [M3 Expressive for Wear OS](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2025/08/introducing-material-3-expressive-for-wear-os.html)
- **Pebble (64 colours, or black and white on the Duo models):** "Take advantage of colors to convey additional information without any text," but "avoid any colors used in places they may have a pre-conceived meaning which does not apply." Colours are "small in number" and sometimes "indicate state, such as the temperature." Reviews of Pebble Time 2 describe its 64 reflective colours as muted and low in contrast (3rd-party, seen only in search snippets). [Recommended](https://developer.rebble.io/guides/design-and-interaction/recommended/), [Core Experience](https://developer.repebble.com/guides/design-and-interaction/core-experience/), [Building for every Pebble](https://developer.rebble.io/guides/best-practices/building-for-every-pebble/)
- **Night colour by context.** Apple Ultra's Night Mode turns the Wayfinder and Modular Ultra faces red, and since watchOS 10 "uses the ambient light sensor to automatically activate in the dark." The display can drop to "just one nit so as not to disturb people close by." [Ultra 2 newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2023/09/apple-unveils-apple-watch-ultra-2/), [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/watch/faces-and-features-apde9218b440/watchos)

## 5. Always-on / ambient: the hard constraints

| Platform | Lit-pixel limit | How it is measured | Update rate | Other rules |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wear OS (WFF, enforced at Play review) | ≤15 % | Average luminance: white = 100 %, black = 0 %, RGB interpolated linearly, checked about every 10 min across a whole day | Typically once a minute | Memory: 10 MB ambient, 100 MB interactive |
| Samsung (Watch Face Studio) | ≤15 % OPR, "can be rejected" above | White 100 %, red 33.3 % | Seconds hidden | Low-bit mode: only cyan, magenta, yellow, red, green, blue and white, no gradients |
| Fitbit (SDK 4.1, needs permission) | ≤20 % OPR | OPR | Clocks once per minute, apps once per second | Sensors stopped; no numeric stats; outlines or checkerboard only |
| Zepp OS | ≤10 % of pixels lit | Pixel count | No second hand | Black #000000 background, white hands, 1–2 px black outline on hands, ≥2 px stroke on digits |
| Huawei | ≤10 % or ≤20 % non-black, depending on resolution | Count of non-#000000 pixels | — | Active face flagged if >50 % of pixels are "overly bright" (R>168, G>168, B>128) |
| Android Wear 2014 (old) | <10 % non-background; colour ≤5 %; "keep 95 % of the pixels black" with burn-in protection | Pixel count | Once a minute, "do not show seconds" | Low-bit: 8 colours; outlines instead of fills; hollow analog centre |

Sources: [Wear app quality WO-P7/P8](https://developer.android.com/docs/quality-guidelines/wear-app-quality), [WFF ambient](https://developer.android.com/training/wearables/wff/ambient), [Samsung states](https://developer.samsung.com/one-ui-watch-tizen/watch-face/states.html), [WFS always-on](https://developer.samsung.com/watch-face-studio/user-guide/always-on.html), [Fitbit SDK 4.1](https://dev.fitbit.com/blog/2019-12-19-announcing-fitbit-os-sdk-4.1/), [Zepp spec](https://docs.zepp.com/docs/watchface/specification/), [Zepp screen-off](https://docs-testing.zepp.com/docs/next/designs/specifications/screen-off-mode/), [Huawei FAQ](https://developer.huawei.com/consumer/en/doc/content/watchface-faq-0000001174035539), [Android Wear doc](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/refs/heads/lollipop-mr1-release/docs/html/design/wear/watchfaces.jd).

- **Apple publishes no pixel quota.** "The system determines the screen's overall luminance by comparing the ratio of lit pixels to dark pixels" and then dims. Apple suggests "replacing large blocks of bright content with stroked outlines and dimmed interiors, as shown on the Numerals Duo watch face," and dimming or removing non-essential information. [Apple: Designing for Always On](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchos-apps/designing-your-app-for-the-always-on-state)
- **Apple: keep the layout and let motion settle.** "Maintain a consistent layout. Avoid making distracting interface changes when Always On begins or ends." "Aim to make infrequent, subtle updates." "Gracefully transition motion to a resting state; don't stop it instantly." Some of Apple's own faces change shape for always-on: on Metropolitan the numerals "rotate to become pills when your wrist is down"; on Pride Harmony "blocks dim and become lines." → Apple accepts a designed always-on form, as long as the change is continuous. [HIG Always On](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/always-on), [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/watch/faces-and-features-apde9218b440/watchos)
- **Apple update cadence in always-on:** TimelineView drops from `.live` to `.seconds` or `.minutes`, and "the system may request views up to 5 minutes in the future." Apps with a background session refresh "about once per second." Time-relative text and progress views "change to a reduced fidelity mode." → Content should be computable ahead of time, for example "x min ago". [Updating watchOS apps with timelines](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchos-apps/updating-watchos-apps-with-timelines), [WWDC22](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10050/)
- **Samsung:** "Do not simply lower the brightness level of an active state to create an always-on state." Use black or dark grounds, avoid high brightness and chroma over large areas, and keep analog centres and pivots "empty or dark". [Samsung states](https://developer.samsung.com/one-ui-watch-tizen/watch-face/states.html)
- **Wear OS app ambient rules (also apply to faces):** keep at least 85 % black, "Use outlines for large icons or buttons rather than solid fills," avoid "non-functional branding or background images," keep elements in the same position across modes, and in low-bit mode disable anti-aliasing and avoid gradients. [Wear OS Always-on](https://developer.android.com/training/wearables/always-on)
- **Default always-on faces exist.** If a face has no always-on version, Samsung and Fitbit show a system clock, and Fitbit also does so when the face lacks the AOD permission. [Samsung states](https://developer.samsung.com/one-ui-watch-tizen/watch-face/states.html), [Fitbit Display API](https://dev.fitbit.com/build/reference/device-api/display/)

## 6. Seconds

- **Most platforms drop seconds in always-on.** Android Wear (old): "Only show hours and minutes in ambient mode; do not show seconds." Zepp: "Don't use the second hand." Samsung's clock components hide seconds in AOD automatically. Fitbit clocks refresh once a minute in AOD. [Android Wear doc](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/refs/heads/lollipop-mr1-release/docs/html/design/wear/watchfaces.jd), [Zepp spec](https://docs.zepp.com/docs/watchface/specification/), [Samsung OPR blog](https://developer.samsung.com/sdp/blog/en/2024/05/07/optimizing-watch-face-battery-usage-by-reducing-on-pixel-ratio)
- **Hardware is changing this.** Series 5 (2019) introduced always-on on an LTPO display; press coverage reports refresh down to 1 Hz (3rd-party). Series 10 (2024) went "from once a minute to once a second" in always-on: "users can now see a ticking seconds hand without raising their wrist on select watch faces." → Whether seconds survive always-on depends on the device generation. [Series 10 newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2024/09/introducing-apple-watch-series-10/), [Series 5 newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2019/09/apple-unveils-apple-watch-series-5/), [TidBITS](https://tidbits.com/2019/09/10/apple-watch-series-5-introduces-always-on-display/)
- **Seconds as design material on Apple faces:** Flux "fills the screen with color second by second"; Palette's colours "change as the second hand sweeps"; Mickey's foot taps once per second, synchronised across watches; Exactograph's seconds dial "expands on tap"; Activity Digital has a "Seconds" toggle. → Seconds can be shown on request, as a texture, or by hands. [Series 10 newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2024/09/introducing-apple-watch-series-10/), [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/watch/faces-and-features-apde9218b440/watchos), [Wired 2015](https://www.wired.com/2015/04/apple-watch-design/)
- **Battery cost of seconds (Pebble):** many faces tick every second but redraw once a minute, so use `MINUTE_UNIT` and "offer users a way to turn off the second hand tick." Core Devices' own watchface skill says: "NEVER use SECOND_UNIT unless the user explicitly requests a seconds display." [Conserving Battery Life](https://developer.rebble.io/guides/best-practices/conserving-battery-life/), [coredevices/pebble-watchface-agent-skill](https://github.com/coredevices/pebble-watchface-agent-skill)
- **Apple's guidance for apps:** when the cadence drops, hide hundredths, then seconds, and show "the time remaining to the nearest minute." [Timelines](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchos-apps/updating-watchos-apps-with-timelines)

## 7. Complications and slots

- **Apple's families (WidgetKit):** accessoryCircular, accessoryCorner, accessoryRectangular and accessoryInline. Inline is drawn "according to system-defined coloring and font." Gauge styles: closed for "a percentage of a whole", open for when the "minimum and maximum values are arbitrary", and segmented for "rapid value changes". Rectangular slots also feed the Smart Stack (152×69.5 pt at 40 mm to 198×81.5 pt at 49 mm). Infograph holds up to 8 complications and Modular Ultra 7. [HIG Complications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/complications), [HIG Widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets), [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/watch/faces-and-features-apde9218b440/watchos)
- **Third parties can't make Apple faces.** They supply complications and widgets, and can share configured system faces (Gradient, Infograph…) with their complications preloaded: "If people add your watch face but haven't installed your app, the system prompts them to install it." Migicovsky: "apparently you can't even write your own watchfaces for Apple Watch?" [HIG Watch faces](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/watch-faces), [Why We're Bringing Pebble Back](https://ericmigi.com/blog/why-were-bringing-pebble-back/)
- **On Wear OS the source sends data and the face decides how it looks.** "The data source plays no part in determining how the data should be represented"; the face chooses how to show `GOAL_PROGRESS` 2400/10000, as text or as an arc. Types: SHORT_TEXT, LONG_TEXT, RANGED_VALUE, GOAL_PROGRESS, WEIGHTED_ELEMENTS, MONOCHROMATIC_IMAGE, SMALL_IMAGE, PHOTO_IMAGE and EMPTY, each with an optional burn-in-protection icon. Slots can be bounded by a rectangle, an oval or an arc along the edge. Google's advice: support several types per slot, put RANGED_VALUE/GOAL_PROGRESS on edge arcs, and render each combination of optional fields well. Maximum 8 slots (WO-P10). [WFF complications](https://developer.android.com/training/wearables/wff/complications), [Wear complications design](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/wear/guides/surfaces/complications), [Wear app quality](https://developer.android.com/docs/quality-guidelines/wear-app-quality)
- **Wear OS complication refresh is capped.** The minimum `UPDATE_PERIOD_SECONDS` is 300 s, and push updates should not come "more often than every 5 minutes on average"; requests come less often in ambient or off-wrist. For time-based values, use time-dependent text or timelines instead of frequent pushes. → A 1-minute CGM reading can't be pushed into a Wear OS complication every minute within these rules. [Expose data to complications](https://developer.android.com/training/wearables/exposing-data-complications)
- **Samsung:** complication kinds are text, text+icon, icon, gauge and "hands" (a value shown by a hand). Content should fit the face's concept ("step count or heart rate suits a sporty face"). Edge slots come in Duo and Quadrant layouts in Watch Face Studio (from a search summary of Samsung's WFS complications guide; page not opened). Keep complications "below eight". [Samsung complications](https://developer.samsung.com/one-ui-watch-tizen/watch-face/complications.html), [WFS design optimization](https://developer.samsung.com/codelab/watch-face-studio/design-optimization.html)
- **Pebble has no complication system.** Each face reads data itself (HealthService, weather through PebbleKit JS) and lays it out freely. → Total freedom, and every face reinvents its data display. [Pebble developer docs index](https://developer.repebble.com/llms.txt), [Core Devices skill](https://github.com/coredevices/pebble-watchface-agent-skill)
- **Huawei:** health data (steps, HR, sleep, stress…) "must be placed in their containers" so that a tap opens the right app. A container that is too large (466×466 HR) steals taps from others. [Huawei FAQ](https://developer.huawei.com/consumer/en/doc/content/watchface-faq-0000001174035539)

## 8. How users customise faces

- **Apple's on-watch editor:** touch and hold the face, tap Edit, swipe through pages (Style, Colour, Dial…; complications are on the last page), tap a slot and turn the Digital Crown to pick, press the crown to save. Per-face options are small sets: Utility has Color, Style, Typeface, second-hand colour and 3 complications; California has Color, 6 numeral styles and Full Screen or Circular. Faces can be shared through Messages and the web. [Apple Support: add complications](https://support.apple.com/guide/watch/add-complications-to-the-watch-face-apd8d0b9c582/watchos), [faces and features](https://support.apple.com/guide/watch/faces-and-features-apde9218b440/watchos), [HIG Watch faces](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/watch-faces)
- **Wear OS:** "Offer customization options… color ways, customizable hands and complications." WFF `UserConfigurations` (colour, list and boolean options) appear in the system Watch Face Editor. Play listings must show "more than one of the available permutations" of a customisable face. [Watch faces](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/wear/guides/surfaces/watch-faces), [WFF configuration](https://developer.android.com/training/wearables/watch-faces/configuration), [Wear app quality WO-G6](https://developer.android.com/docs/quality-guidelines/wear-app-quality)
- **Android Wear (old):** settings on the watch "should be limited to binary selections or scrollable lists"; complex settings belong on the phone. Pebble does the same: settings are a phone-side Clay page with colour pickers. [Android Wear doc](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/refs/heads/lollipop-mr1-release/docs/html/design/wear/watchfaces.jd), [Pebble developer docs index (tutorial part 6: Clay settings)](https://developer.repebble.com/llms.txt)
- **Preview conventions:** Zepp previews must show 10:09, and its default preview data is 10:09:36, HR 86 bpm, 8670 steps and battery 75 %. → Store previews are standardised, so a face is judged first in its default state. [Zepp spec](https://docs.zepp.com/docs/watchface/specification/)

## 9. Stale, missing and private data (including health)

- **Wear OS: replace stale live values.** "Any data that changes frequently — such as a stopwatch, heart rate… becomes stale between updates. To avoid displaying misleading and incorrect information, replace these live values with static placeholder content, such as `--`." Also "remove progress indicators that require frequent updates." When a dynamic value can't be evaluated (for example off-wrist), the platform shows the invalidation fallback inside `NoDataComplicationData`. A timeline falls back to a default value ("No event"). [Wear OS Always-on](https://developer.android.com/training/wearables/always-on), [Expose data to complications](https://developer.android.com/training/wearables/exposing-data-complications)
- **Fitbit:** "Clocks should not display stats as numbers [in AOD], to avoid misleading users with stale data." [Fitbit SDK 4.1](https://dev.fitbit.com/blog/2019-12-19-announcing-fitbit-os-sdk-4.1/)
- **Pebble:** cache the last successful load in persistent storage and tell the user how old the data is. [Recommended](https://developer.rebble.io/guides/design-and-interaction/recommended/)
- **Zepp:** empty values are written "0", "- -" or "--" (heart rate and temperature use "- -"). [Zepp customization: watchface](https://docs.zepp.com/docs/designs/customization/watchface/)
- **Apple:** each complication needs static placeholder images "when there's no other content to display." Redaction uses a redacted version of the placeholder. There are four states to design: active, redacted, low-luminance and redacted low-luminance. [HIG Complications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/complications), [WWDC22](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10050/)
- **Apple on health data is not consistent.**
  - Developer doc: "always hide any highly sensitive information, such as financial information or health data" in Always On.
  - HIG: "Hide sensitive information… like bank balances or health data," but "keep other types of personal information glanceable when it makes sense… pace and heart rate updates while they're working out."
  - WWDC22: on watchOS, content is "not redacted in low luminance" by default; users can turn redaction on, and `.privacySensitive()` marks the views (the example is heart rate).
  - Ways out are given: "If people don't want any information to be visible, they can turn off Always On."
  - [Designing for Always On](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchos-apps/designing-your-app-for-the-always-on-state), [HIG Always On](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/always-on), [WWDC22](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10050/)
- **Wear OS "Privacy first":** "The watch travels with the user everywhere they go. Consider how the content in the complications is relevant to the user's context." [Wear complications design](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/wear/guides/surfaces/complications)
- **Fitbit gallery:** apps that use "sensitive health information" or aid "treatment, or prevention of disease… may be subject to additional review," and the developer must substantiate health claims. Clock faces are exempt from Fitbit's design guidelines. [Fitbit App Gallery Guidelines](https://dev.fitbit.com/legal/app-gallery-guidelines/)

## 10. Touch on faces

- **Minimum touch targets:** Apple 44×44 pt default and 28×28 pt minimum, with about 12 pt of padding around bezelled elements and about 24 pt around unbezelled ones. Wear OS 48×48 dp (WO-V2). [HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility), [Wear app quality](https://developer.android.com/docs/quality-guidelines/wear-app-quality)
- **A tap can do something without leaving the face.** On Wear OS, "tapping a Water Count complication changes the glass count" (a self-contained action); app-launch shortcuts are provided automatically. Apple gives each complication its own deep link. Apple faces also use taps: Wayfinder's edge becomes a compass, and Chronograph Pro's edge "transforms into a chronograph." → A face can be an input surface, not only a display. [Wear complications design](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/wear/guides/surfaces/complications), [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/watch/faces-and-features-apde9218b440/watchos)
- **Touch works in ambient on modern Wear OS and Apple Watch.** "Interactive UI elements such as buttons can remain visible because modern Wear OS devices support touch input during ambient sessions." On Apple Watch, controls stay interactive in Always On. [Wear OS Always-on](https://developer.android.com/training/wearables/always-on), [Designing for Always On](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchos-apps/designing-your-app-for-the-always-on-state)
- **Pebble is button-first** ("one click away"), with touch only on Time 2 and Round 2. The Core Devices manifesto: buttons "to play/pause/skip music… without looking at the screen." [Core Experience](https://developer.repebble.com/guides/design-and-interaction/core-experience/), [Why We're Bringing Pebble Back](https://ericmigi.com/blog/why-were-bringing-pebble-back/)

## 11. Motion, and faces that react to context

- **Apple's motion principles:** "Add motion purposefully… Don't add motion for the sake of adding motion." "Make motion optional… avoid using it as the only way to communicate." Animation should be brief, cancellable and rare on frequent interactions. On watchOS, layout animations have built-in easing that can't be turned off. WWDC15: "Animations on Apple Watch should, of course, be quick." Haptics should be used "sparingly to draw people's attention to truly important events." [HIG Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion), [WWDC15](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2015/802/)
- **Wear OS Material 3 Expressive:** "meaningful movement, such as spring animations and shape morphing." In ambient: "Pause or remove all animations… including animated progress indicators and scrolling marquee text." [M3E for Wear OS](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2025/08/introducing-material-3-expressive-for-wear-os.html), [Wear OS Always-on](https://developer.android.com/training/wearables/always-on)
- **Animation only when data needs it (Samsung).** Samsung's example makes a blink run only when heart rate is outside 60–100 BPM (`([HR]<60)+([HR]>100)?…`), because "a component that blinks every second forces a refresh each second." → Motion can be gated on a data condition. [WFS design optimization](https://developer.samsung.com/codelab/watch-face-studio/design-optimization.html)
- **Pebble:** animate "to draw the eye to updated or changing information." Play animations at smart moments, "when a tap or wrist shake is detected," and let users reduce or disable them. [Recommended](https://developer.rebble.io/guides/design-and-interaction/recommended/), [Conserving Battery Life](https://developer.rebble.io/guides/best-practices/conserving-battery-life/)
- **Apple's context-reactive faces show the range of triggers:**
  - Sun position: Solar Analog, Solar Dial, Solar Graph.
  - Weather and workouts: Snoopy and Woodstock "react to the weather conditions… get active when the user does a workout."
  - Wrist motion: Flow "swirls in response to movement"; Reflections "reacts subtly to user movements."
  - Ambient light: Night Mode.
  - Time passing: Contour "gradually changes to highlight the current hour."
  - Raise or tap: Motion shows a different flower each time; Artist changes on each tap.
  - Content: Siri Modular shows "the top Smart Stack widget for that moment."
  - [watchOS 10 newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2023/06/introducing-watchos-10-a-milestone-update-for-apple-watch/), [Series 10 newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2024/09/introducing-apple-watch-series-10/), [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/watch/faces-and-features-apde9218b440/watchos), [Wired 2015](https://www.wired.com/2015/04/apple-watch-design/)
- **Apple Smart Stack relevance moments** are a catalogue of triggers: an imminent time, a location reached, headphones detected, waking up or going to bed, and the start or end of a workout. [WWDC23](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10309/)

## 12. Battery cost of design choices

- **OLED power depends on colour.** "OLED displays consume dramatically different power for showing different colors" and "are inefficient for showing bright colors"; a colour-adapted scheme cut display power by 64 % in the cited study (smartphones, 2011). [Dong & Zhong, Chameleon (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1101.1240)
- **Samsung's measured example:** hiding the background, date and second hand in AOD took OPR from 13.4 % to 2.4 %. Other cost drivers named: light canvases (an off-white #fbf5f5 "can significantly increase the OPR"), gyro effects, oversized images, and hidden components (which "are still partially rendered"). Shapes are cheaper than bitmaps. [Samsung OPR blog](https://developer.samsung.com/sdp/blog/en/2024/05/07/optimizing-watch-face-battery-usage-by-reducing-on-pixel-ratio), [WFS design optimization](https://developer.samsung.com/codelab/watch-face-studio/design-optimization.html)
- **Huawei flags overly bright active faces,** even outside AOD, when more than 50 % of pixels exceed its brightness threshold. [Huawei FAQ](https://developer.huawei.com/consumer/en/doc/content/watchface-faq-0000001174035539)
- **Pebble (reflective memory LCD, no emission cost):** the cost is CPU wake-ups. A face "with animations that play every second" can cut Pebble Time Round battery to "one day or less"; an always-on backlight lasts "a few hours". Core Devices targets about 30 days for Time 2 (3rd-party summary of the launch post). Fitbit's Clock API stops ticks while the display is off. [Conserving Battery Life](https://developer.rebble.io/guides/best-practices/conserving-battery-life/), [Introducing two new Pebble watches](https://ericmigi.com/blog/introducing-two-new-pebbleos-watches/), [Fitbit Clock Face Guide](https://dev.fitbit.com/build/guides/clockfaces/)
- **Wear OS moved rendering to the system** (WFF) "so you can focus on your creative ideas, rather than code optimizations or battery performance." [WFF overview](https://developer.android.com/training/wearables/wff)

## 13. What each platform forbids or discourages

- **Apple:** third parties can't create watch faces. Discouraged: thin font weights, colour as the only signal, full-screen colour on long-lived views, distracting always-on changes, health data visible in always-on (see the conflict in §9), lines under 2 pt.
- **Wear OS (enforced):**
  - Since January 2026 only WFF faces install: declarative, no app code (`hasCode="false"` for Watch Face Push).
  - Ambient ≤15 %, at most 8 slots, the time must always be shown (WO-V12), memory 10/100 MB.
  - Play listing: the icon must be the circular face itself; screenshots show only the face, 1:1, with more than one permutation.
  - Watch Face Push lets a companion app install validated WFF faces from a "marketplace".
  - [WFF overview](https://developer.android.com/training/wearables/wff), [Wear app quality](https://developer.android.com/docs/quality-guidelines/wear-app-quality), [Watch Face Push](https://developer.android.com/training/wearables/watch-face-push)
- **Samsung:** AOD above 15 % OPR; dimming the active design as the AOD; bright, high-chroma areas; bright analog centres. A forum report says faces above 15 % can't even be sent to one's own watch (unverified). [Samsung states](https://developer.samsung.com/one-ui-watch-tizen/watch-face/states.html), [Samsung forum](https://forum.developer.samsung.com/t/unable-to-send-face-to-watch-because-of-aod/33982)
- **Fitbit:**
  - AOD only with the `access_aod` permission; such faces "cannot be sideloaded" and can't be published without Fitbit's authorization.
  - In AOD: ≤20 % OPR, sensors stopped, no numeric stats on clocks, only outlines or checkerboard fills.
  - Incorrect AOD "can… cause physical damage to the display."
  - The legacy Fitbit Web API is turned off on 30 Oct 2026, a sign of the platform's sunset.
  - [Fitbit Display API](https://dev.fitbit.com/build/reference/device-api/display/), [SDK 4.1](https://dev.fitbit.com/blog/2019-12-19-announcing-fitbit-os-sdk-4.1/), [Gallery guidelines](https://dev.fitbit.com/legal/app-gallery-guidelines/)
- **Zepp:** in AOD, more than 10 % lit pixels, a second hand, a non-black background, and gradients, highlights or shadows on hands. Important information in the top and bottom bands. Faces must support English and both Chinese scripts. [Zepp spec](https://docs.zepp.com/docs/watchface/specification/)
- **Pebble:** `SECOND_UNIT` without a user request, thin edge rings on round screens, hardcoded 144×168 geometry, and continuous text reflow on round screens. [Core Devices skill](https://github.com/coredevices/pebble-watchface-agent-skill), [Round App Design](https://developer.rebble.io/guides/design-and-interaction/in-the-round/), [Building for every Pebble](https://developer.rebble.io/guides/best-practices/building-for-every-pebble/)

## 14. Designers and design history

- **Alan Dye (then Apple's human-interface chief; left Apple in December 2025 per [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Dye), seen only in a search snippet):**
  - On craft: the Motion faces are "all in-camera… the longest one took us 285 hours, and over 24,000 shots."
  - On Activity rings: "We spent a year, and did far more studies… Different ways that, at a glance, someone could understand that information… and hopefully in a really simple and visceral way feel like they accomplished something when they fill them up." Wired adds that "a not-quite-complete circle… drives you just crazy enough to take those last 400 steps."
  - Why it matters: progress shown as an emotional shape, not a number.
  - [Wired 2015](https://www.wired.com/2015/04/apple-watch-design/)
- **Faces borrowed from horology are tool-watch idioms built for legibility.**
  - California copies the WWII "error proof"/"high visibility" dial (Roman numerals on top, Arabic numerals below, an inverted triangle at 12).
  - GMT copies the Rolex GMT-Master; Count Up copies a dive bezel; Chronograph Pro copies a Speedmaster or Daytona.
  - [arun.is: iconic watches behind Apple faces](https://arun.is/blog/apple-watch-faces/) (3rd-party), [9to5Mac California](https://9to5mac.com/2019/06/20/watchos-6-customizing-california-apple-watch-face/)
- **What made Pebble faces loved.** More than 16,000 faces were on the Pebble store, made possible by a hackable SDK and an always-on, sunlight-readable reflective screen ("Not distracting to others like a bright wrist"). The constraints (144×168, 1-bit, 20 KB RAM) produced a recognisable pixel aesthetic. TTMM: "A discipline that small allowed designs that visually large"; "These designs don't pretend to anything, they don't simulate anything. They simply are themselves." [Why We're Bringing Pebble Back](https://ericmigi.com/blog/why-were-bringing-pebble-back/), [TTMM archive](https://ttmm.is/pebble-watch/)
- **2025–26 Pebble revival:** Pebble 2 Duo (144×168, black and white), Pebble Time 2 (200×228, 64 colours, touch, RGB backlight) and Pebble Round 2 (260×260, 64 colours, touch). Core Devices publishes an AI-agent watchface skill whose defaults are minute ticks and brief animation, and a phone-side settings page. There is still no new formal design guide beyond the original Pebble guides. [Core Devices skill](https://github.com/coredevices/pebble-watchface-agent-skill), [Round App UI](https://developer.repebble.com/guides/user-interfaces/round-app-ui/)

## Contradictions and open questions

1. **Health data in always-on.** Apple's developer doc says always hide health data. The HIG says keep workout heart rate glanceable. WWDC22 says nothing is redacted by default. Wear OS asks only for context awareness. No platform addresses medical data that the wearer acts on, such as glucose.
2. **Stale numbers.** Fitbit forbids numeric stats in AOD clocks, and Wear OS replaces live values with `--`. Apple allows complications in always-on, renders them ahead of time and lowers the fidelity of relative times. A CGM value updated every 1–5 minutes falls between "live" and "stale" in all three models.
3. **The always-on budget differs by a factor of four or more** (5–10 % in Android Wear 2014, 10 % Zepp, 10/20 % Huawei, 15 % Wear OS and Samsung, 20 % Fitbit), and the measurement differs (Wear OS averages luminance; Huawei and Zepp count non-black pixels; Samsung weights by RGB).
4. **Seconds in always-on** are banned on most platforms but sold as a feature on Apple Series 10. This is a hardware question (LTPO3 / 1 Hz), not a design consensus.
5. **Black and minimal vs expressive.** Wear OS says "use the color black" and also "Expressive". Apple's HIG discourages full-screen colour on long-lived views, yet Apple ships Flux, which fills the screen with colour every second.
6. **One message vs many slots.** Android Wear 2014 says "stick to one message", while Infograph and Wear OS allow 8 slots. Both are official.
7. **Colour as information.** Pebble encourages colour that carries meaning without text; Apple forbids colour as the only carrier because of tinted mode. These can be reconciled with redundant shape or position, but the emphasis differs.
8. **Reacting vs stability.** Android Wear asks faces to change style and colour with data, and Apple ships faces that react to weather, light and movement. Apple's always-on guidance asks for "a consistent layout" and "infrequent, subtle updates."
9. **Minimum text sizes don't translate** (12 pt Apple, 12 sp Wear OS, 18 Pebble, 22 px Zepp). Physical size depends on pixel density, which none of them state for faces.
10. **Gaps.**
    - The web-search budget ran out near the end, so these weren't reached: Hodinkee Radio transcripts (2019, 2020), the Wired "secret history" piece, current Samsung Wear-OS-era design pages (Samsung's design pages are Tizen-era), and Huawei's 10 % vs 20 % resolution table.
    - Fitbit's design-guidelines page is an empty placeholder ("We're still updating these guidelines for… Fitbit OS 5.0").
