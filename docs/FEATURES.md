# Sukrara behaviour catalog

The features and exact behaviours of every Sukrara app. One line per behaviour, with the rule and the numbers; the
"why" stays in the investigation reports (`docs/NN-*.md`).

Rule: every change that adds or changes a behaviour updates this file in the same commit.

**Complete flows** (CLAUDE.md; user, 2026-10-21: "There's no way to unmute!"): every feature section has a
**States and exits** paragraph: each state a user can enter, where it is visible, and how to change it and leave it
(undo, cancel, turn off, unmute, go back) on every platform and surface that shows it. `tools/check/run.sh` (check
`states`) fails on a section without one unless `tools/check/allow.json` `states_pending` lists it with the reason.

## Alerts

Rules: [contracts/alerts/SPEC.md](../contracts/alerts/SPEC.md); timelines: [contracts/alerts/scenarios](../contracts/alerts/scenarios/).

- Every change to the alert rules adds or updates a scenario in `contracts/alerts/scenarios/*.txt`; Android
  `AlertScenarioTest` and the Mac `ScenarioTests` (`AlertsTests`) both run all of them and must pass (SPEC §7). Mac: the pure
  `SukraraAlerts` engine runs them in `AlertsTests` (`cd apps/macos && swift test --filter AlertsTests`, one test case
  per scenario from `contracts/alerts/scenarios`).
- **LO / HI** (Android and Mac, SPEC §1): a sensor's "below / above my range" reading (`sgv < 40` / `> 400`) matches
  every low / every high kind whatever the thresholds, so the most severe one turned on (very low, extremely high)
  alerts at once instead of waiting for *No glucose data*; never a falling-high prediction from a HI, and a LO / HI is
  never "back in range by the margin" (a Mute / Remind less of that side holds). Alert texts say
  *LO · below 40 mg/dL* / *HI · above 400 mg/dL* (es *por debajo de 40* / *por encima de 400*), never 39 / 401, and
  the Nightscout upload and the xDrip+ endpoint compute no slope from or to one (scenarios `levels.txt`).
- Kinds and defaults (the user's settings): very low < 55 every 5 min (alarm volume), low < 70 every 5 min, high ≥ 180
  every 15 min, very high ≥ 250 every 10 min, extremely high ≥ 300 every 5 min (alarm volume), falling/rising fast
  2 mg/dL/min every 30 min (off), no data after 20 min every 60 min; other kinds at notification volume.
- Lows are strictly below the threshold (70 → alerts at 69); highs at or above it.
- "Very low" (id `very_low`) replaced "urgent low" everywhere (settings JSON, channels, sounds, events); no migration.
- Level alerts: one at a time, the most severe wins; a worse kind of the same side alerts at once; getting better
  (very low → low) doesn't alert.
- Rate alerts (falling/rising fast) run next to level alerts; a reading without a rate (a LO / HI has none) keeps an
  open falling / rising fast as it is (alert up, repeat due, a Mute not back by it): a LO isn't a sign the fall stopped; no data runs on a timer and clears on the next reading. The Android timer is an elapsed-time alarm, so a clock change (`TIME_SET`) re-checks at once and plans it again (round 22: with the clock moved forward the old high stayed up and no "no data" came; a real gap alerts on time, scenario *A high with no more readings…*).
- Readings older than 15 min never alert; the alert clears when the reading gets that old.
- Episodes: one per group and side; a brief exit (back in range for less than the repeat interval, at least 5 min)
  clears the notification but keeps the episode, its repeat count and its treatments; no new "first" alert.
- Repeats count from the last alert of the episode; repeat 0 = once per episode.
- Logging the answering treatment clears the alert; when its mute window ends and it still matches, it alerts at once
  and the repeats count from there.
- Carbs mute very low, low and falling fast (default 15 min); rapid insulin mutes high, very high and rising fast
  (default 60 min); no treatment mutes extremely high or no data (the user's own Mute / Mute all do); window = `t ≤ now < t + min`; deleted entries don't count.
- Notification actions (round f30, SPEC §2; the user 2026-10-21: "two kinds of buttons … Mute 30 min doesn't conceptually belong there"; Android guidance: up to three actions that don't duplicate the tap, developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/build-notification#Actions, choices via RemoteInput): every alert notification, every kind (very low and extremely high included), has exactly **Mute…** and **Remind less** (Mac *Remind Less*) on Android and Mac. Mute… is one action whose choices are the lengths (Android direct reply: 15 min / 30 min / 1 h / 2 h and the setting's own length among them, shortest first, plus typing; TalkBack hears each chip as what it does, *Low alerts: mute for 30 min* / es *Alertas de glucosa baja: silenciar durante 30 min*, through a `TtsSpan` on the chip, whose text stays the reply (#103, `AlertMuteChoicesTest`); Mac text input, placeholder *30 min, 1 h, 2 h…*, empty = the setting); typed text goes through the shared duration parser (`45`, `1 h 30`, `1:30`, `2h`; 1–720 min); anything else changes nothing and the notification comes back silently. Remind less (es *Avisar menos*; was *Relax*) repeats the side every `max(repeatMin, relaxMin)`; it shows only when it would slow anything (no data, every 60 min, gets Mute… alone), and once relaxed the slot is **Remind as usual** (es *Avisar como siempre*); the interval is in the secondary text (*Reminders every 30 min*), never on the button. After Mute… Android replaces the alert with a silent *Low muted until 10:42* with **Unmute** and **Change…**; macOS removes a notification once an action is used and posts a passive one with the same two. Since macOS removes it on every action, the Mac posts an alert that is still up again without sound after *Remind Less* / *Remind as Usual* (now with the other button; nothing ringing came back meanwhile) and after a *Mute…* / *Change Time…* reply that isn't a length (unchanged; the muted note when the side is muted), for its own alerts and the wearer's mirrored ones (`AlertControllerTests`). Scenarios: `contracts/alerts/scenarios/actions.txt`. Android: the rules are the pure `:core:alerts` module (`AlertMachine`: inputs → effects Fire / Clear / Back / Mirror / Schedule; persisted `AlertState` incl. the wearer's shared run so a restart never re-rings followers; a restart alerts at once for whatever came due while the app was down; a follower's Mute / Unmute act on their side only — a high's Mute never hides a showing low; a clock set back keeps repeats, windows and mutes as far away as they were, and a reading dated in the future counts as taken now).
- Scope and end of Mute / Remind less (round f30; the user: "a high mute … then goes low, the low would be muted, and we don't want that"): they act on one side (lows incl. very low; highs incl. extremely high; falling fast; rising fast; no data), never the other. They cover very low and extremely high too (the user's decision; treatment mutes still never cover extremely high). They outlive the episode and end at their time (Mute) or once back in range **by the margin**: lows ≥ low threshold + 10 mg/dL, highs < high threshold − 10 mg/dL, for 10 min in a row (`notificationActions.backMgdl` / `backMin`, settings rows *Mute and Remind less end when back in range by* / *… for at least*); crossing back out restarts the count, so oscillation at the edge stays quiet. History: *Low back in range: mute and relax ended*.
- **Mute all** (round f30; the user: "a global mute button with a time option … Never is also an option"): Android home control **Mute alerts** (es *Silenciar alertas*), Mac dropdown **Mute Alerts**: 30 min · 1 h · 2 h · 4 h · 8 h · **Until you turn it off** (es *Hasta que lo desactives*, after AOSP Do Not Disturb; was *Never*) · Custom… (the one number input, 5 min–24 h, schema `muteAll`, whose special 0 is `until_turned_off`; was `never`). It quiets every kind, very low, extremely high and no data included, on that device only (the wearer still sends the followers' alerts); alerts showing go. When it ends, at its time or with *Unmute* (also a minute after muting), whatever still matches alerts at once, however recent its last alert (history *Alerts back on* / *Alerts unmuted*; scenario *Unmute (Mute all) right after muting*). It survives a restart.
- Notification actions and Mute all are per device (user 2026-10-21: "if someone wants to mute, it shouldn't mute the other"): only that device's notifications, pill and history change; nothing is sent. The wearer runs its rules twice (its own notifications with its actions, the followers' `alert` events without). On a follower mirroring the wearer (phone or Mac, `AlertMirror` on both) they drop the wearer's alerts there (while relaxed a repeat shows only after `relaxMin`; a more severe kind always shows) and end by the same time and margin rules on the readings that device gets. With own rules they act on that device's engine. Treatment mutes are shared data and quiet every device. Scenarios: `devices.txt` (`mirror`).
- Badge (`AlertStatus`, both platforms, `contracts/graph/status.json`): a side's Mute (or Mute all) out of range = the tonal pill with the bell and time left in the colour of what answers it (*Muted* when Mute all has no end); a treatment mute shows first. Mute all in range = the neutral **muted** pill with the "notifications off" bell, *Muted · 1h 20m* / *Alerts muted* (short *Muted*), on every card surface (app, widgets, glucose notification, Mac dropdown and widgets); the Mac menu bar puts a bell.slash before the value.
- Durations (round f30; the user: "Showing 60 min and 120 min is long, 1 h and 2 h is better"): every duration in words goes through one formatter per platform (Android `:core:model` `Durations.kt`, Mac `SukraraCore/Durations.swift`, cases `contracts/settings/durations.json`): *45 min*, *1 h*, *1 h 30 min*; the card keeps its compact *1h 4m*. tools/check rule `durations` fails on a duration written by hand.

- Falling-high mute (default 20 min): no high/very high alert while `sgv + rate × min < high`; it only postpones (a
  repeat that came due fires when it ends).
- Headlines: "Very low glucose", "Low glucose", "High glucose", "Very high glucose", "Extremely high glucose",
  "Falling fast", "Rising fast", "No glucose data"; body "69 mg/dL ↘ · < 70 mg/dL".
- Headlines acknowledge treatments logged in the episode (or within the mute length before it): lows "· eat more"
  (1 carbs entry) / "· eat more again" (2+); highs "· still high after insulin" / "· still high after more insulin"
  (never advise more insulin). Counted even when the mute is off.
- The wearer's `alert` event carries `tr` (that count) so followers and the Mac show the same headline.
- Notification body (Android + Mac): the value and arrow only (`69 mg/dL ↘`); with no reading behind the alert (Android) *No recent reading*, never a made-up value; falling / rising fast add the rate (`120 mg/dL ↓ · falling 2.4 mg/dL/min`); no data: `Nothing received for 20 min · last 120 mg/dL`. No threshold, no source ("from the wearer's phone").
- Android timer: exact alarm (`USE_EXACT_ALARM`) at the engine's next check, so a mute end alerts on time between
  readings; Mac: one-shot timer at the next check plus the 60 s tick.
- Badge status (`AlertStatus`): None / Needed / Taken / Paused use the same thresholds and treatment windows as the engine.
- Notification channels: two per kind. Android ids `alert.<kind>` (notification volume, `USAGE_NOTIFICATION_EVENT`, group *Glucose alerts*) and `alert_alarm.<kind>` (alarm volume, `USAGE_ALARM`, passes "Alarms" in Do Not Disturb and overrides a mode that silences alarms once Do Not Disturb access is granted: Android applies `setBypassDnd` only with that access, so the checklist creates the channels again as soon as it is granted (on the return to the app), keeping every choice the person made; group *Glucose alarms (alarm volume)*); the person sees them as *Very low*, *Low*, … under those two groups in System settings › Notifications. Ids never change (renaming resets the person's choices). (PoC: `alert_n_<kind>`, `alert_a_<kind>`.) Every alert that can ring posts as `CATEGORY_ALARM` so Android 15+ *notification cooldown* (per app: a second sounding notification within 60 s at 30 % volume, a third within 10 s muted; only alarms are exempt) never quietens one — Low and Falling fast fire in the same reading. A notification-volume alert keeps `CATEGORY_REMINDER` only while Do Not Disturb would treat the two differently (Alarms only, Priority without both alarms and reminders), so DND lets it through exactly as before (`AlertCategory`, `AlertCategoryTest`, `AlertNotificationAttributesTest`). Android also makes only one sound per app per second, so when one reading fires several alerts the one to hear is posted first (alarm level, then the most severe; the others show without sound; `AlertRingOrderTest`), while the phone is unlocked and Android's *Vibrate only when unlocked* is on (the person's answer *Keep it on* in the checklist), Android only vibrates, so Sukrara plays the alert channel's own sound itself, once, on the alert's volume, only for a ringing post whose channel sounds and vibrates and only where Do Not Disturb lets the alert through; a Clear or Mute stops it (`UnlockedAlertSound`, `UnlockedAlertSoundTest`; also for *Send a test alert*); and the app's other notifications that would sound (sensor, status, requests, pens) wait 1.1 s and post only 1.1 s after the last alert sound, so a notice of the same moment (the sensor lost right before *No data*) never mutes the alert (`SoundGap`, `SoundGapTest`; the *Monitoring stopped* notice posts at once: Android is stopping the service). Debug builds: `ALERTS --es kinds low,falling_fast --el gapMs 2000` posts made-up alerts through the notifier (docs/15 has the check).
- Android runtime (`:monitor` `AlertRuntime`): one alert runtime per process owns the engine; the monitoring service feeds it (hub state, settings, treatments) and the exact alarm (`AlertCheckReceiver`; boot, app update, clock and time-zone change through `BootReceiver`) ticks it **with the service dead**, from the saved record (episodes, mutes, Mute all, the last reading, the sensor's life, whether monitoring started): *no data* still alerts on time when Android killed the service, then the service is asked to restart. Both paths share the one runtime, so an alert never posts twice. Effects are applied before the state is saved (a crash in between rings again rather than resuming silently); each effect step (the alarm first, then notifications, followers, saving, publishing) is tried on its own, so a refused notification never leaves the exact alarm unarmed or the state unsaved; a failed step is written to the diagnostic log (no values) and listed in the hub's alert state (`failedSteps`) until it works again: the alert checklist reads it, so home shows *Alerts may not ring* · *Android refused an alert step* with *Copy report* (the failed steps, the missing items, the app and Android versions; nothing personal), and Settings › Alert reliability lists *Alert delivery* with *Copy report* while it lasts; an alert Android refused to post is posted again, with its sound, on the next input. Boot, app update and clock changes check alerts and start the service once (`BootReceiver`). The alert check uses `setExactAndAllowWhileIdle`, not `setAlarmClock` (that would show an alarm icon and replace the person's wake-up alarm as the phone's "next alarm"); with exact alarms refused it falls back to an inexact while-idle alarm. **No phantom alarm**: before the first reading (or with no sensor / monitoring stopped) *no data* is off and nothing is scheduled; without a role nothing runs. Alerts are never bundled with other notifications: every alert and muted card is in Sukrara's own *Glucose alerts* notification group with its summary (Android 16 force-groups ungrouped and sparse groups; 0.11.53), the summary never rings and goes with its last alert. Alarm-volume kinds (very low and extremely high by default) also open over the lock screen with a full-screen intent when Android allows it; whether an alert may not reach the person has one answer, the alert checklist (`AndroidAlertReadiness`, below under Home screen), which home, Settings › Alert reliability and setup all read. A swipe only removes that notification: the episode keeps its repeats.
- Android alert delivery (`:monitor`): every alert channel's sound is `content://app.mauri.sukrara.alertsound/<kind>` (`AlertSoundProvider`, exported read-only because System UI's player opens it): the file is picked at play time (the pack chosen in the app, a random variant), and a file that can't be opened falls back to the system sound, never silence; a sound picked in the channel's system settings wins. One vibration per kind (`AlertHaptics`, so the alert is recognisable in a pocket: lows fall and thud, highs rise, no data ticks away), composed from primitives, with an on/off waveform of the same rhythm where primitives aren't supported. The notification's actions follow the side's state: an alert whose repeat isn't faster than *Remind less* shows only *Mute…*; a relaxed side shows *Remind as usual* (with its "reminders every …" subtext). No per-side notification light colour (most phones have no LED).
- One sound per alert: each channel rings only its Sukrara sound; the app restores its episodes after a restart (an
  update kills it) and shows the alerts that were up again without sound; the next sound is the normal repeat
  (Android + Mac: Mac UserDefaults `alertEpisodes`, restored at launch with the last stored reading; history/log line
  "resumed High (no sound)"; scenario event `restart`, SPEC §7). Mac: the engine snapshot (open episodes, the sides'
  Mute / Remind less, Mute all; format `v` 1) is stored apart from the settings (SPEC §3 JSON under the schema key
  `alerts`); an unreadable or other-version snapshot starts fresh (alerts on, nothing muted), Mutes already over are
  dropped and Mutes longer than the longest choice are cut to it; a clock set back never stalls the repeats.
- Default sound pack: Quest, a different variant each time, no per-kind packs; followers mirror the wearer by default.

## Treatments

- Amounts are written lowercase with no space before the unit everywhere (Android + Mac): `4.5u`, `18u`, `30g` (dialog suffixes, lists, chart labels, the slot; also the Nightscout API's long-acting note; the Mac's treatment rows and amount field).
- Short durations read `now` (< 1 min), `12m`, `1h 5m` (whole hours: `3h`), only hours from 6 h (`7h`), days from 48 h (`2d`); time left rounds up (`1m` until it is over).
- The home treatments list (wearer) and the follower's list: each row starts with the time of day, then the amount as the card writes it and the kind (*30g carbs*, *4.5u rapid insulin*), and ends with the time since the entry in the card's short durations (`12m`, `3h`), refreshed on home's minute tick. An entry an insulin pen logged reads *8.5u rapid insulin · from the pen* (on every role: a follower stores the entry's pen keys as received, `TreatmentWire.penJson`) and the whole row is one button (TalkBack: *show its doses*; its Delete stays a button of its own) that opens **the pen entry sheet** (`:core:designsystem` `pen/PenEntrySheet`, the same sheet as *Doses read*): the amount and time, *From the pen · 1.5u primed*, each dose with *Priming* / *Injected*, the note and the entry it replaced; *Save* corrects it (snackbar *Entry changed* / *Removed from your log* with *Undo*; *This entry changed meanwhile. Nothing was saved.* when a read or another phone changed it; *Couldn't undo* when it changed after), *Delete* is home's own delete (snackbar *Undo*), *Done*. On a follower (or a phone without pens) the doses are read only (their role in words) and only a writer's own entry has *Delete*. Mac: Treatments window rows end with the time of day and the same `12m` / `3h` (refreshed every 30 s while shown).
- The treatment dialog (Android + Mac) focuses carbs instead of rapid insulin when the latest fresh reading is below an enabled low-side alert threshold (`AlertStatus.level` in `Kind.LOW_SIDE`); otherwise rapid.
- The treatment dialog on Android (home screen and `QuickTreatmentActivity`): the Rapid / Long / Carbs labels always sit small on the field outline on one line, with the unit (`u` / `g`) shown throughout, so no field and not the dialog changes height when the focus moves (`TreatmentDialogHeightTest`, en + es, 360 / 412 dp).
- The treatment dialog's time (Android + Mac): *When?* switches between a clock time and *Minutes ago* (Android: M3 segmented button, a number field with 5 / 15 / 30 / 60 min filter chips; Mac: segmented control, field + buttons). Minutes ago are 0–1439 (under a day, like the clock time; OK disabled when empty or 1440+, the field outlined red), 0 = now; the chip shows *16 min ago* (es *Hace 16 min*) and the entries get the time 16 min before the moment of saving, not of picking. The quiet line counts from that time. Cases in `contracts/treatments/treatment-form.json` (`minutes`, `minute_filters`); `TreatmentTimeTest` (also at 411 dp).
- The treatment dialog's form (Android + Mac, `TreatmentForm`, cases in `contracts/treatments/treatment-form.json`): insulin rounds to 0.1u (≤ 100u), carbs to whole grams (≤ 500g), `,` or `.` as decimal mark; 0, or 0 after rounding, is invalid; one invalid field blocks Save; a blank note adds nothing, a note is trimmed and clipped to 100 bytes.
- Writer scope (investigation 24; Android + Mac; `WriterScope`): a follower allowed to add entries (a writer) creates entries and changes or deletes **only the ones it created**; the wearer changes any. The wearer's phone refuses a writer's change to the wearer's own or another writer's entry (logged `treatment REJECTED: … may only change its own entries`; no echo, so the writer's copy stays *not accepted yet*). Followers apply the same rule: each entry's creator is the key that signed its first accepted version; a writer's copy that arrives after the wearer's echo of it is ignored, not counted as rejected; the wearer's own entry replaces a copy a writer forged first, whatever its version.

## Sensor life

- Android reads two sources (`:sensor`, `SensorProfile`): **FreeStyle Libre 3 / 3 Plus directly** (NFC start / takeover, then Bluetooth; 60 min warm-up and 15 d life when the patch info doesn't say; a sensor's own patch info wins) and **Juggluco's local web server** (Juggluco owns the sensor), plus five families **never tested on a real sensor** (`SensorProfile.verified = false`; decoding pinned by open-source recorded frames in `contracts/sensor/traces`): **Dexcom G7 / ONE+** and **G7 15 Day / Stelo** (read along with the Dexcom app on a G7 this phone is bonded with, writing nothing to the sensor; 30 min warm-up, 10 / 15 d), **Dexcom G6 / ONE** (keys from the typed 6-character transmitter id; needs a session started by the Dexcom app or receiver; 2 h, 10 d), **Accu-Chek SmartGuide** (the standard Bluetooth CGM Service, bonded with the cap's PIN in Android's dialog; 60 min, 14 d) and **Aidex X / LinX / GlucoRx** (keys from the typed 10-character serial; 60 min, 15 d). These are found by short filtered scans (`ble/SensorScanner`: 30 s on, 60 s off, by service UUID or exact name, never unfiltered; Nearby devices covers it, no location), the found address is kept and later connections go straight to it over the same `GattLink` (indications, reads, write-without-response, a bond that waits 2 min for the PIN). After 10 empty scans the status says *not found* (check the id) and scanning goes on. Value rules (`brand/BrandDriver`): only the sensor's own number in its range (Dexcom and SmartGuide 40–400 mg/dL); below / above it the reading is the flagged **LO / HI** (`Reading.lo` / `Reading.hi`, stored and shared as 39 / 401 like Nightscout and xDrip+, no rate): the card says *LO* / *HI* in the range colour and the alerts treat it as beyond every threshold (see *Alerts*). The same for every family: Libre 3 (the capped word's range flags, below → LO and above → HI, when the numbers don't contradict them, and otherwise uncapped and capped value both below 40 → LO, above 400 → HI; a data-quality error, too hot / too cold included, a flag the numbers contradict or the no-range code is no value; a HI keeps the sensor's number for LibreView; `contracts/sensor/traces/libre3.txt`, #132), Dexcom, SmartGuide (−∞ with its *below range* warning → LO, +∞ with *above range* → HI; NaN or a contradiction → no value), Aidex X. A field that isn't a measurement is no value, never a LO / HI: Dexcom 1–12 (old special codes) and the all-ones 0xFFF, Aidex outside 18–800 (Juggluco); stored backfill records keep only in-range numbers; timed by the sensor's own clock (G6 by the transmitter clock from its time reply, Aidex by a start that is kept once known); a value from the future is dropped, one older than 3 min is history, and the same value again after a restart is history (never the current value the alerts read). A wrong id / refused authentication / failed PIN closes the link (*Pair the sensor again*) and is kept with the pairing (`Pairing.failed`): a restart, reboot or Bluetooth toggle never retries it in the background (no PIN dialog out of nowhere), only a new pairing does. Before a new pairing ever reached its sensor, the first device that refuses the id (G6: it advertises only "Dexcom" + the id's last two characters) or the PIN (SmartGuide: every one advertises "AC-…") may be another person's sensor that the scan found first (#133): it is skipped (`Pairing.strangers`, never picked again for this pairing; a new pairing starts without them, since a corrected id may be for that very device) and the scan runs once more (one 30 s window); a second refusal, or no other sensor in that window, fails the pairing as above. An Aidex X advertisement only ever adds a reading: one heard between two polls with a warm-up flag, no value or a status byte never seen with a value (no public map of it: Juggluco leaves it a TODO) leaves the link's state as the polled record set it (no flicker). A Dexcom G7 that is already past its wear when this pairing first sees it (an old sensor still bonded, ended or in its grace period) is never adopted and never ends the new pairing: its address joins the pairing's skip list (`Pairing.skipAddresses`, at most 8; a new G7 pairing after an ended one also skips the ended sensor) and the scan goes on; Dexcom states other than *ok* give no value (calibration, temporary issue, sensor error); a Dexcom end state ends the sensor for good; a SmartGuide "session stopped" is confirmed with a status read and shown as *no session*, never as the end (its end is start + 14 d).
- The Libre 3 source (`sensor/libre3/Libre3Driver` over `sensor/ble/GattLink`): connect by the address from the NFC tap with autoConnect (no scanning), one GATT operation at a time. Protocol ported byte for byte from the PoC (handshake, fresh pairing after a scan, resume with the saved authorization, every notification subscribed, 120 s glucose wait after authorizing, 24 h backfill, warm-up wait), pinned by `Libre3DriverTest`. Reliability: every GATT operation and the service discovery time out after 10 s; a dropped or failed link is closed at once and a **new** connection is made after 1 s, doubling to 30 s while it keeps failing, back to 1 s once the sensor sends data; Bluetooth off closes everything with no retries (state *Bluetooth is off*), Bluetooth on reconnects at once with a new connection; callbacks of a closed connection are ignored; a revoked Nearby devices permission stops the link with a state that asks for it. No reading for 10 min on a running sensor (not warming up, not ended) restarts the link (`SourceManager` watchdog). After 3 authorization rejections in a row (the sensor's PIN / key changed: another app took it over) it stops reconnecting and says *another app has the sensor* (*Try again* / scan it again).
- NFC commands to the Libre 3 (`sensor/libre3/Libre3Nfc`) are retried up to 10 times within 5 s on a transient error or an answer with the error flag (Juggluco's `wholenfccmd`); a lost tag (phone moved away) aborts at once and the scan screen asks to try again.
- The owner knows the current sensor's start, end and warm-up end (`core/SensorLife`, persisted): the direct source gives serial, start, end = start + the wear length from the NFC patch info (LE16 at +6, minutes; Libre 3 Plus 21600 = 15 d; the profile's 15 d when unknown or for a pairing saved before this field) and warm-up end = start + the patch info's warm-up (byte 13 × 5 min; the profile's 60 min default).
- Juggluco source, API secret (optional; schema `source.jugglucoSecret`, the Keystore SecureStore `juggluco`, never in a settings file): when set, every request (history, `sgv.json`, the `/x/live` stream) carries `api-secret: sha1hex(secret)` as Juggluco and Nightscout clients expect (the Mac sends the same); it is read again at every connection. Juggluco answering 401 / 403 is `JugglucoStatus.Refused`: no readings, retried with the usual backoff, and Settings › Sensor says Juggluco refuses the secret. The secret authenticates Sukrara to Juggluco, not Juggluco to Sukrara: another app holding the port can still answer.

## Sensor scan: other sensors

- **Android**: the Libre 3 receiver-ID rules below are in `:sensor`; the other sensors' scan is not included (see *Not included*). A Libre 1 / 2 tag (TI chip) shows *Libre 1 and 2 sensors aren't read here* (nothing sent; start and read it with Juggluco) with *Use Juggluco instead*; another Libre-made sensor (a Lingo, unreadable patch info) *This isn't a Libre 3*; any other tag (a card, an NFC sticker) *That wasn't a sensor* with *Try again* (`PairOutcome.NotASensor`).
- The New sensor screen's NFC tap checks the chip first (UID maker byte): only 0x7A (Abbott, Libre 3 / 3 Plus) gets Libre 3 commands. 0x07 (Texas Instruments: Libre 1 / 2 / 2 Plus) gets no command at all and shows *Libre 2 sensors aren't supported* — start it with Juggluco, then pick Juggluco as the glucose source — with a *Glucose source* button; any other tag → *Not supported* / "not a FreeStyle Libre 3".
- Receiver ID (`sensor/pairing/ReceiverIds`, `ReceiverIdPolicyTest`): a Libre 3 is bound for its whole life to the receiver ID it was started with; a takeover (`A8`) works only with that ID, a wrong one is refused (`B1`) and changes nothing. Starting a never-started sensor (patch state 1, `A0`) uses only the LibreView account's receiver ID (from *Sign in to LibreView*: LibreLinkUp login, read-only, stores the receiver ID and the email — never the password or a token), so Juggluco or Abbott's apps on the same account can take it over later; without it **nothing** is sent and the flow asks for the sign-in. A takeover tries the account's ID, then Sukrara's own (a random ID made once; it can't succeed in practice because Sukrara never starts a sensor with it); without the account ID nothing is sent either. Sign-out forgets the account's ID; Sukrara's own ID and the paired sensor stay. Every successful tap replaces the saved pairing (the sensor gives a new PIN) and the next Bluetooth connection pairs afresh.

## Root status bar (system icons)

- Android (`apps/android/monitor` `systemui/`; Settings → Status bar → *With root*): *Value in the status bar* (off by default; only someone who turns it on is asked for root) with a `slot:` placement (Placement: *With the system icons* = leftmost, *After the VPN icon* / *Bluetooth icon*, *Before the mobile signal* (the default: left of the more permanent mobile / Wi-Fi / battery), *After the mobile signal* / *Wi-Fi icon*) shows the reading as Android system icons, set by the root slot daemon (`SlotDaemon`, run with `su` through `app_process`, `IStatusBarService.setIcon`): two slots of its own, `sukrara_glucose` (the number, `sb_n…` / struck `sb_s…` / `sb_lo` / `sb_hi`) and `sukrara_glucose_trend` (the arrow every 5°, double from ±3, the ring, `sb_t…`); no readings / sensor ended = one icon in the trend slot (number slot removed); warming up = none. What they show is `GlucoseCard.face` for `status-icons` (`SlotIcons`, `SystemUiTest`). SystemUI draws and tints them; with the LSPosed module they get the range colour (out of range only; struck values keep SystemUI's tint). Their TalkBack text starts with *Sukrara glucose* (English: System UI reads it to recognise them), never matching another app's icons. The daemon never leaves a stale value: it clears its slots when it starts, when the app lets go or dies (EOF), when it crashes, and after 10 min without a word from the app (the app pings every minute and sends every reading and freshness step); it accepts only its own slots. The app talks to it on one background thread with timeouts (30 s for the root prompt, 5 s per reply), newest state only. A failed start (root refused, no answer) is retried after 5 min, doubling to 30 min (at once after a settings change or a System UI hello). The value never vanishes from the bar: while a `slot:` placement's icons aren't on screen (no root, the daemon failed) the module draws the text after the clock instead, and Sukrara's notification icon is hidden only while the value shows (text or icons). *Value in the status bar* stays switchable while on, so it can be turned off after root was revoked; root is asked only when it is turned on (and on the page while it is on).
- The LSPosed module (`SystemUiHook`, libxposed API 102, `META-INF/xposed`, scope System UI) draws the value as text after / before the clock or left of the system icons (the clock's font, tint and fading; range colour when on; the card's arrow or ring; struck at half strength from 5 min; the "no readings" icon from 20 min; the flag once ended; a warm-up's time left), keeps our icons ordered, coloured and first in line, and hides Sukrara's notification icon (*Hide Sukrara's notification icon*, on by default; the app re-posts its notification after the module's hello so it applies at once). It re-renders every 30 s by itself. It is fed by a broadcast only the app can send (signature permission + the sender's identity checked) with every value clamped. Safety: the whole entry and every hook catch everything (PROTECTIVE hooks; a failing piece turns itself off), at most 40 log lines a minute, kill switch `adb shell setprop debug.sukrara.systemui_off 1` (or `settings put global sukrara_systemui_off 1`) then restart System UI.
- **mmol/L**: the system icons are drawables of the mg/dL numbers (`sb_n40` … `sb_n400`), so with the glucose unit in mmol/L the app shows no icons and the module writes the value as text in their place (`HookState.unit`, the same text placement as without root).
- **Glucose wins the space (LSPosed module, 2026-10-22).** Android 17 lays the icons out right to left and turns the first one that doesn't fit, and everything left of it, into one overflow dot; ours are external icons, so they were the leftmost and the number went first, leaving a lone arrow when vibrate / hotspot icons appeared. The module hooks SystemUI's `EllipsizingRow` measurement (`hook/SystemUiHook.hookIconFit`, logic in `hook/IconFit`, `IconFitTest`): SystemUI's own fit runs with our icons first in line, then the shown icons go back to their on-screen order. Other icons fold into SystemUI's dot (from the left, in SystemUI's order) before ours; number and arrow are one unit: both shown, or both inside the one dot (never one without the other) when even the pair alone doesn't fit. The placement only decides where they sit. Our icons are recognised by their TalkBack text (`RootStatusBar.isOurDescription`). If SystemUI's code doesn't match, the hook logs `icon fit … failed` and SystemUI's own behaviour stays.
- *Before the mobile signal* (`slot:<mobile`): right before the mobile icon; with mobile hidden (no SIM, Wi-Fi only), where it would be: after the last shown icon ranked before mobile in `config_statusBarIcons` (`IconFit.insertBefore`). On the user's Pixel that order is … speakerphone … vpn, bluetooth … mute, volume (vibrate), zen … hotspot, stacked_mobile, mobile, wifi, airplane, battery, so the pair lands after vibrate and never between mobile and Wi-Fi. Same on the lock screen status bar (the same icon list).
- **Without the module** (root only, or the module not in System UI's scope / not loaded, per `SystemAccess`): every `slot:` placement still shows the icons, as plain system icons first among them (SystemUI's own position, no priority, gap or order fixes); the Settings rows say so (*Before the mobile signal needs the LSPosed module. For now, it shows first among the system icons.*) and those placements stay selectable in the dialog with that line under them. The clock / left-of-icons placements show nothing without it, so they stay disabled with *Needs the Sukrara LSPosed module*. No root: the switch is unavailable, as before.
- "After X" while X is hidden (Bluetooth off, no VPN): SystemUI's list only has the shown icons, so ours go where X would be, by the platform's slot order (`config_statusBarIcons`): after the last shown icon that comes no later than X (`IconFit.insertAfter`). Before, they jumped to the far left.
- **Number and arrow at the card's gap (LSPosed module, 2026-10-22; the user: "closer, less spacing").** SystemUI puts its 6 dp icon spacing between our two slots (5.74 dp measured). The module brings the trend to the card's own number → arrow gap, so every surface reads alike: the renderer's geometry (`ValueGlyphs` `MARK_GAP` 0.08, `MARK_BOX` 0.62, `ARROW_IN_BOX` 1.05 of the text size, the arrow's outline) with the card's font, ink to ink, averaged over the digits and the 5° angles = 0.27 of the digits' height (`IconFit.CARD_GAP_OF_DIGIT`, recomputed by `IconFitTest`); at the status bar's 13 sp icons (digits 28 px at 420 dpi) that is 8 px ≈ 3 dp instead of 16 px. How: the measure hook makes the row that many px narrower and SystemUI's own placement block (hooked) moves the trend and everything after it left by as much, so the gaps to the neighbours and the dot stay SystemUI's; each icon is still placed once by SystemUI. Those px go to the other icons (2026-10-22; the user saw the dot where vibrate would fit): SystemUI's fit is offered the row's width plus the pull (its own measure Constraints, widened through Compose's `Constraints.copy`), so an icon that fits once the pair is closer shows instead of the dot. Ours are fitted first, so either both fit, side by side, and the pull takes the px back (the row ends within the real width), or they fold into the dot together and nothing else fits either (`IconFit.pulledRow`, a 20,000-row check in `IconFitTest`). Only when the number sits right before the trend in SystemUI's order (the order hook) and the gap hook is in place; SystemUI's fitting rules are otherwise unchanged. The trend slot's TalkBack text is *Glucose trend ↗* (the number's stays *Glucose 120 mg/dL ↗*), which is how the module tells them apart. Only left-to-right layouts, only when number and trend are shown side by side; anything unexpected → SystemUI's spacing (`icon fit … failed`). Root alone keeps SystemUI's spacing (nothing can move a system icon without the module).
- **After a System UI restart (2026-10-22; the user: the arrow showed on the left, then jumped).** system_server hands System UI our two icons back in hash order, trend first. With the module, the order hook always shows number then trend (`IconFit.arrange`), with every placement including the plain one and before the app's state arrives, so the pair is right from the first frame. The slot daemon's own fix (re-adding both icons 8–13 s after it sees a new System UI) was that visible jump and a remove / add blink, so it is skipped when the module said hello in that System UI with its order hook in place (the app sends `order module` to the daemon; keyed by System UI's pid, so a restart without the module re-adds again).
- **Root without the module**: after a System UI restart the arrow can show left of the number until the daemon re-adds them (8–13 s). The placement is always leftmost and SystemUI fits the row on its own, so a crowded row can still drop the number and keep the arrow. Not fixed with one combined icon per (value, trend): the Compose status bar ignores the icon level, so that needs 14,883 drawables (363 values × 40 marks + struck), measured +6.4 MB on the release APK.
- Not done: hiding the mobile network type (LTE / 5G) or the signal bars to make room. SystemUI hides the type only when mobile data isn't the active connection; hiding it while on mobile data drops real information without any sign, whereas the overflow dot is the platform's own "more icons" signal.

**States and exits**: *Shown* (value pair, or the single no-readings / ended icon): turn it off in Settings → Status bar → *Value in the status bar*, or move it with *Placement*. *Folded into the overflow dot* (the row is too narrow even for the pair, or other icons win without the module): the value stays in the glucose notification and the app; the dot is SystemUI's own and goes away as soon as there's room (an icon leaves, or another placement). *After a hidden icon*: shown where that icon would be; it moves next to it when the icon appears. *Just after a System UI restart*: with the module, number then trend at once; without it, swapped until the daemon re-adds them (seconds, by itself). *Module missing or failed*: a `slot:` placement shows first among the system icons, SystemUI's own fit; the *Value in the status bar* and *Placement* rows name the placement and say it needs the LSPosed module and shows first for now; Settings → Status bar says what's missing and home's *Restart System UI* card reloads the module; leave it by enabling the module (then the placement applies by itself) or by choosing *With the system icons*. A clock placement without the module shows nothing: its rows say it needs the module and the dialog offers the system-icon placements (selectable) to leave it. All of it reachable with TalkBack (Settings rows; the icons read "Glucose 120 mg/dL ↗", "Glucose trend ↗"). The closer gap is not a state: it follows the module (on with it, SystemUI's spacing without it).

## Monitoring (service, restarts, stopped)

Android (`:monitor`: `GlucoseHubImpl`, `MonitoringService`, `MonitorStarter`, `MonitorReceiver`, `HealthCheck`;
tests `GlucoseHubTest`, `MonitorServiceLogicTest`).
- One `connectedDevice` foreground service for both roles hosts the role's one reading source; START_STICKY.
- Started from: opening the app, boot, app update, time / time-zone change, the **health check** (an exact wake-up
  every 15 min on the monotonic clock that restarts the service when there is something to monitor), the alert check, and the *Resume* notice. Nothing starts when setup isn't done or nothing is set up to monitor.
- **Monitoring starts at the first reading** of the current source (remembered across restarts). Before that every
  surface says *Not monitoring: no sensor yet* and no "no data" alarm can be armed. Only removing the
  sensor or changing the role (explicit actions) go back to *Not monitoring*.
- Readings: LO / HI kept (never dropped) and stored as the flags at the one way in (Android hub `GlucoseRange.normalise`,
  Mac follower `GlucoseRange.normalised`): any number below 40 from any source (sensor, Juggluco, relay, Nearby) is
  stored as 39 and any above 400 as 401, without a rate, so the follower's card shows *LO* / *HI* and the graph, uploads
  and the CSV export (39 / 401, never "LO" / "HI": Tidepool's LibreView import refuses a non-numeric cell) all agree;
  0, negatives and values above 1000 are dropped as malformed; a reading stamped more than 5 min ahead of the phone's clock is dropped
  (it would look fresh for too long); the gap then shows and alarms as no data, and the hub says the phone's clock is
  behind (`GlucoseState.clockBehind`, #50: home's *Phone clock is behind*) until it accepts a reading at least as new
  (older backfill doesn't clear it). The hub keeps the last 24 h for every surface.
- Bluetooth off → the wearer sees *Bluetooth is off*; on → the source reconnects with a fresh connection.
- **Monitoring stopped**: when Android refuses to start the service (background start not allowed, permission) the
  hub says Stopped and a notification on `status` says *Monitoring stopped* · *No glucose readings or alerts until it
  runs again. Tap to resume.* with *Resume*.
- **States and exits**: *Running* → (Android kills it) → START_STICKY / the health check bring it back within 15 min.
  *Stopped* → tap the notice or *Resume* (starts it; a notification tap is an allowed start), or open the app; the
  notice goes away once it runs. The in-app banner for Stopped / Not monitoring (visible without notifications) is the
  home screen's. *Not monitoring* → *Set up sensor* / finish setup → first reading.
- After a restart, monitoring resumes at the **first unlock**, not before: see
  [Before the first unlock](#before-the-first-unlock-android).

## Before the first unlock (Android)

Android (`:monitor` `lockedboot/`: `LockedBootPlan`, `LockedBootStore`, `LockedBoot`, `LockedBootReceiver`; `App`;
tests `LockedBootPlanTest`, `LockedBootTest`, `LockedBootReceiverTest`, `LockedBootPlanSaveTest`, `ManifestTest`;
investigation [docs/30](30-direct-boot.md)).
- After a restart (an overnight OS update, a crash, the phone's own restart after days locked) a phone with a screen
  lock keeps the app's storage encrypted until the person unlocks it once. Readings, the sensor pairing, the
  identity and the alert state live there, so **Sukrara doesn't monitor before the first unlock**: no Bluetooth, no
  relays, no readings, no level alerts.
- What it does instead, from a small **device-protected plan** (`locked-boot.json`: whether monitoring had started,
  the last reading's *time*, *No data*'s minutes / re-alert / sound, whether any alert that is on rings as an alarm,
  *Mute all*'s end and the sensor's life; never a glucose value), which the alert runtime rewrites with every save:
  - at the locked boot, if a role is set and monitoring had started: a **lock-screen notice** on `status`, public and
    silent, *Unlock to resume monitoring* · *Your phone restarted. Sukrara can't read your sensor or alert you until
    you unlock it.*;
  - when *No data* would have rung (the last reading + its minutes; later while the sensor warms up or *Mute all* is
    on; never once the sensor has ended or with *Mute all* until turned back on, *No data* off or alerts off), the
    notice **rings as an alarm**: *No glucose data · unlock your phone* · *No readings since 3:10. Sukrara can't read
    your sensor or alert you until you unlock your phone.*, on *No data*'s channel at alarm volume when any alert that
    is on rings as an alarm (very low by default: every alert waits for the unlock), else at notification volume;
    *No data*'s sound pack; again every *No data* re-alert interval (0 = once). A restart long after the last reading
    rings at once.
- Nothing shows on a phone without a screen lock (Android unlocks it by itself) or when nothing was set up to monitor.
- No full-screen alarm, *Mute…* or *Remind less* before the unlock (those open the app or need its storage).
- **States and exits**: *Waiting for the first unlock* (notice on the lock screen) → unlock (or tap the notice, which
  asks for the unlock and opens the app): the notice and its alarm go at once and monitoring starts as after any
  restart (alert check, service). *Ringing* → swipe it away (it comes back at the next re-alert) or unlock. Turning
  *No data* off (Settings › Alerts) before a restart leaves only the silent notice. TalkBack reads the notice on the
  lock screen like any notification.

app (Alerts › States and exits). *Add treatment* (tap) → *Cancel* / Escape closes it with nothing saved. A widget is
removed or resized from the desktop's *Edit Widgets* (system). No widget state blocks anything.

## Sensor reminders and countdowns

Wearer only (`sensorend/`; rules in `SensorEndPlan`, tests `SensorEndPlanTest`); followers get none of these. Settings → **Sensor end**.
- **Android** (`:monitor` `sensorend/`: `SensorEndPlan` pure rules on virtual time, `SensorEndNotifier`,
  `AndroidSensorEndPresenter`; page `:feature:settings` `sensorend/SensorEndPage`; settings `sensor.reminders` JSON +
  `sensor.countdownHours`; tests `SensorEndPlanTest`, `SensorEndNotifierTest`). Differences from the description
  below: reminders and *Sensor ended* use channel `sensor`; both countdowns and *Sensor ready* (silent) use
  `sensor.countdowns`; every one of them is in Sukrara's info group (`g:app.mauri.sukrara.INFO`, with its summary),
  the countdowns too (#156: with the glucose notification, the one ungrouped notification, they went into Android's
  automatic bundle; a group child can still be a Live Update); the sensor's start is derived from its end (15 d) or ready time (60 min); a sensor is a new one
  when the source names another one (its serial: Libre 3 / Juggluco; the debug synthetic source makes a new one per
  `SYNTHETIC`), so one replaced on its first day gets its own warm-up countdown, *Sensor ready* and reminders; a
  source without a serial falls back to a start and an end that both move by more than a day. Swiping a countdown away goes to `AlertCheckReceiver` (kept away for that
  sensor). States and exits: each reminder (tap opens the app, swipe); countdowns (swipe = gone for this sensor, or
  the switches on the page); *Sensor ended* and its repeats (*Replace sensor* → new-sensor flow; a new sensor clears
  it and stops them; *Remind again* · *Off* on the page; *Forget this sensor*); turning a switch off removes what it
  shows on the next re-plan.
- Reminders: a list of (days before the end day 0–3, time of day), at most 6; default *1 day before at 09:00* and *on the end day at 09:00*. Each fires in the phone's time zone on (end day − days) at its time, only if that is after the sensor's start and before its end (a sensor ending at 07:30 skips "end day 09:00"). Text: *Sensor ends today / tomorrow / on Wednesday at 14:30* (≥ 7 days: the date) · *Have a new sensor ready.* One notification slot: the later reminder replaces the earlier one.
- Reminders that passed before Sukrara knew them (new sensor, edited list) never fire; one missed while the phone was off fires late if the sensor hasn't ended (of several, only the latest).
- The page lists the reminders, then *Add reminder* as a button under the list (the same dialog, empty; hidden at 6), then the first-unlock switch in its own group.
- **First unlock** (switch *Remind at first unlock*, default on; 0.11.19): a reminder set for the morning (after 05:00, before 12:00) shows at the first unlock (`ACTION_USER_PRESENT`, registered at runtime by the wearer service; it can't be in the manifest) of its day from 05:00, if that comes before its time; otherwise at its time (the exact alarm stays as the fallback). The evening reminder below works the same way. An unlock counts only if it brings a reminder forward (`SensorEndPlan.unlockWanted`; other unlocks do nothing) and only if it is newer than the last reminder / the last settings change. The slot is remembered (`PlanMemory.lastReminder` = its time), so a later unlock, the fallback alarm or a restart never shows it twice; no alarm is left at its time.
- **Night-time end** (group *Night*: switch *Remind the evening before*, default on; *Bedtime* 23:00, *Wake-up time* 07:00, editable with the time picker; Android doesn't share its Bedtime schedule with other apps, so the window is Sukrara's own): if the end falls in the night window (bedtime inclusive, wake-up exclusive; it may wrap midnight), one more reminder at bedtime − 2 h (21:00) the evening before that night (for an end at 03:10 on Wednesday: Tuesday 21:00), or at the first unlock from bedtime − 4 h (19:00) if earlier: *Sensor ends tonight at 3:10* · *Change it before bed*. A night-time end means a gap in readings and a no-data alert during sleep. Same channel as the other reminders; not an alarm, not a Live Update (Live Update guide: "Inappropriate uses: … upcoming calendar events").
- **Last day, take the spare**: a reminder shown on the end day for an end later that day outside the night window says *Sensor ends today at 7:08 pm* (the phone's 12/24 h format) · *Take your spare sensor with you*; an end inside the night window keeps *Have a new sensor ready.* (the evening reminder covers it). Wording per the notification design guide (developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/home-screen/notifications): when it ends in the title, what to do in a short text that doesn't repeat it.
- End countdown (default on): an ongoing ProgressStyle notification from end − *Countdown starts* (`SensorLife.countdownHours`, 1–24 h, default 8, shared with the card's countdown ring) to the end: *Sensor ends at 14:30* · *Have a new sensor ready to replace it.*, the bar = time gone in the window (it fills toward the end like a journey's ProgressStyle bar and the warm-up's, #140; a plain bar, no tracker icon at its tip), a chronometer counting down to the end, no action buttons. Silent channel, refreshed every 15 min (non-waking). It takes the place of a reminder shown before its window (that one is removed when the countdown starts; one that comes during the window stays). It goes by itself at the end (`setTimeoutAfter`), so its timer never runs below zero however late the end's alarm is (#140); that timeout sends the swipe's delete intent, which at the end isn't taken for a swipe.
- It is promoted (Live Update chip with the timer) only while the glucose chip is off: the status bar shows one chip and the countdown would take it from the glucose value for hours. With the glucose chip on it stays in the shade and lock screen. On Android 16 without QPR2 (API 36.0, no promoted notifications) both countdowns are plain ongoing notifications (the promotion calls are tried, never required; `SensorEndNotifyTest` posts both on Robolectric's 36.0).
- Warm-up countdown (default on): from a new sensor's start to the warm-up's end (`SensorInfo.warmupEndEpoch`, 60 min), always promoted: *Sensor warming up* · *Readings at 19:45*, the bar fills up (`SensorLife.warmupFraction`), the chip counts down; refreshed every 5 min. **A countdown never goes below zero**: at the warm-up's end (exact alarm) it becomes *Almost ready* · *Waiting for the first reading*, bar full, no timer, until the first reading; it gives up 15 min after the warm-up end without a reading (`SensorTiming.WARMUP_GRACE_S`). The first reading cancels it (in any state) and posts its own *Sensor ready* · *Readings have started.* (sound, gone after 10 min), only if the warm-up countdown was shown and within 1 h of that reading. The first reading's time is remembered per sensor, so a restart (an app update) doesn't bring the countdown back; every re-plan without a warm-up cancels a leftover one. The end countdown likewise stops at the end (exact alarm) and hands over to *Sensor ended*.
- No *Hide* button on either countdown (0.11.20: the user wants no Hide button on any notification). The way out: **swipe it away** (since Android 14 an ongoing notification is dismissible except while the phone is locked and on *Clear all*, developer.android.com/about/versions/14/behavior-changes-all; a Live Update can be "temporarily dismiss[ed] or demote[d] … to a standard notification", developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/live-update), which keeps it away for that sensor through the delete intent (the guide: "Don't repost Live Updates that the user dismissed. Use setDeleteIntent to detect dismissed updates"), the warm-up one then also skipping *Sensor ready*; or **turn it off**: Settings → Sensor end → *End countdown* / *Warm-up countdown*, the system's *Sensor countdowns* channel, or Live Updates for Sukrara in the system settings (`ACTION_MANAGE_APP_PROMOTED_NOTIFICATIONS`).
- At the end (or when the sensor reports it ended early, end moved to now): *Sensor ended* with the action *Replace sensor* (tap or action: the New sensor screen; the card's wording) once, on the reminders channel, at most 12 h after the end; a new sensor clears it. **Remind again** (#140; the user: "every 2h or so (configurable) it reminds me to apply a new sensor"): it comes again (same notification, with its sound) every *Remind again* hours from the last one (group *After the end* on the page, the one number input: *Off*, *Every hour*, *Every 2 hours* (default), *Every 4 hours* or custom 1–12 h; a field of the `sensor.reminders` JSON, its format the schema number `endedRepeatHours`), never in the night window (one due then comes at the wake-up time), at most 3 days after the end, until a new sensor (or *Off*, or *Forget this sensor*). The night window's *Bedtime* / *Wake-up time* stay editable while either the evening reminder or *Remind again* uses them.
- Channels: *Sensor reminders* (default importance, sound) for reminders / ended; *Sensor countdown* (`sensor.countdowns`: default importance so it shows on the lock screen also when it isn't promoted, the normal case with the glucose chip on, and with the phone's "hide silent notifications" option; no sound, no vibration) for both countdowns and *Sensor ready*. It replaced the low-importance `sensor.countdown`, which the lock screen hid as silent; Android can't raise an existing channel's importance, so the old one is deleted (`Channels.DELETED`; `ChannelsTest`, `ChannelsCreateTest`, #148). Not the alert channels.
- Scheduling: two AlarmManager alarms, re-planned on every alarm, new sensor or end change, settings change, boot, app update, clock and time-zone change. The next event (reminders, window start, warm-up end, warm-up give-up, the end, *Sensor ended* again) is always its own `setExactAndAllowWhileIdle` alarm (Sukrara already holds `USE_EXACT_ALARM` for alerts; 10-min window if exact alarms are unavailable); a progress refresh before it is a second, non-waking `setWindow` alarm (it waits while the phone sleeps: #140, a countdown left to a held-back tick ran its timer below zero). The first reading re-plans at once (while the warm-up may be up), and a failing re-plan doesn't stop that watch.
- **Announced once** (#102): what was shown for a sensor (each reminder slot, *Sensor ended*, *Sensor ready*, the
  warm-up, a swiped countdown) is saved (typed DataStore `sensor_end`) **before** it is posted, so a process killed
  while posting loses at most that post and never rings it twice; while the hub knows no sensor (forgotten, or not read
  yet after a restart, an app update or before the first unlock) every notice goes and nothing is planned, but that
  memory stays, so the same sensor coming back never announces a step again (a new sensor starts fresh; a follower,
  *Start fresh* or leaving the role forgets it). Tests `SensorEndNotifierTest` (a restart, a swipe through a restart,
  a post killed half way).
- Settings export / import / reset include the reminders, the first-unlock and evening switches, the night window, both countdown switches, the countdown hours and *Remind again*.
- Debug commands (Android debug builds only, `app/src/debug` `DebugReceiver` / `DebugFakes`, adb only: `adb shell am broadcast -n app.mauri.sukrara/.debug.DebugReceiver -a app.mauri.sukrara.debug.<NAME> …`): `SYNTHETIC --ez on true|false [--ez frozen true] [--ei cycle M] [--ei endsInMin N] [--ei warmupMin W]` (the synthetic source replaces the sensor: a 3-hour curve between 45 and 215 mg/dL, `cycle` puts now at minute M of it, 135 = the lowest point; a sensor ending in N minutes; a new sensor warming up for W minutes), `ALERTS [--es kinds low,falling_fast] [--el gapMs 0] [--ez reminder true]` (made-up alerts through the real notifier, outside the alert runtime), `PEN_READ` and `PEN_TAGS` ([Insulin pens](#insulin-pens-docs29)). Nothing else: the reminders, the end countdown and *Sensor ended* follow the synthetic sensor's end. The release app never shows a glucose value, an alert, a treatment or a sensor state that isn't real: the `src/debug` source set isn't in a release build (`tools/check` rule `debug`; `verify-apk.sh` fails an APK whose manifest or R8 mapping names `DebugReceiver`, `DebugFakes` or `Synthetic*`). The Mac's: `sukrara-snap card|windows|shell <dir>` (`-es` for Spanish) and `SUKRARA_DEV=1 SUKRARA_FIXTURE=1` ([Mac menu and windows](#mac-menu-and-windows)).
- Android permissions and `<queries>` (#36): only what a shipped feature uses, each declared by the module whose code uses it with the reason in its manifest (`:monitor` the monitoring service, notifications, exact / full-screen alarms, battery exemption, calendar; `:share` / `:sensor` Bluetooth; `:feature:sharing` the camera; `:integrations` uploads, the local network, Health Connect and the broadcast receivers' formats; `:pen` NFC and Juggluco). Not declared: Health Connect's controller packages (the platform API needs none), xDrip+ by package (found through the broadcast formats), and androidx.biometric's `USE_BIOMETRIC` / `USE_FINGERPRINT` (removed: the system's Credential Manager sheet asks for the screen lock). The merged list is `tools/check/allow.json` `permissions` / `queries`: `ManifestTest` and `verify-apk.sh` fail on any difference.

## Sensor end in calendar

Android (`monitor/sensorend/SensorCalendar.kt` writes, `app/AppSensorCalendar.kt` keeps it in line, Settings ›
Sensor end › *Calendar* group `feature/settings/sensorend/SensorCalendarGroup.kt`; `SensorCalendarPlanTest`). The
*Sensor ends in …* / *Sensor ended* notifications stay the reminder; the event is for planning a change around the day.

- Setting *Sensor end in calendar* (`sensorCalendar.on`; wearer only, off by default; hidden on a follower). Turning
  it on asks for READ_CALENDAR + WRITE_CALENDAR; refused → stays off with *Off · needs access to your calendar*;
  refused for good → *Off · access blocked, tap to allow in Android's settings* and the next tap opens Sukrara's page
  in Android's settings; coming back re-reads it. A permission revoked later turns the setting off (on the page's
  return; the writer writes nothing without it).
- Summary: *On · <calendar>* or *Off · an event when each sensor ends*.
- Where (`sensorCalendar.calendarId`, *Calendar* row, enabled while on): **Sukrara's own calendar** by default
  (`ACCOUNT_TYPE_LOCAL`, account "Sukrara", brand colour, read-only for the person, created when needed and found by
  account, never twice; it never syncs, so it stays on this phone), or **any writable calendar on the phone** (e.g. the
  person's Google calendar, *syncs to your other devices*), written as a normal app so the account syncs the events and
  their deletions; their ids are kept in `files/state/sensor-calendar.json`. Changing the choice removes the events from
  the old place and writes the current sensor's event in the new one. A picked calendar that is gone (or its account)
  → back to Sukrara's own.
- *Show in Google Calendar* row (on, own calendar, Google Calendar installed): *Google Calendar → Settings → Manage
  accounts → Sukrara*; tapping it opens Google Calendar. Google Calendar hides a non-Google account's calendars until
  the person turns the account on there, and no other app can.
- One event per sensor: *Sensor ends*, *Sensor <serial>, started <date>. Sukrara keeps this event up to date.*, 30
  minutes from the sensor's end (calendar apps draw a block with its title), free, no reminder of its own, one accepted
  organizer attendee for the calendar's owner (no RSVP in Google Calendar). Keyed by serial, so restarts update it. A
  future event follows the end; once its time has passed it stays put. Past sensors' events stay (a history); a
  replaced or forgotten sensor's event still ahead is removed. A picked calendar that is gone: the events are removed
  from it and go to Sukrara's own (a calendar only hidden in a calendar app keeps them).
- Turning it off deletes Sukrara's calendar with its events, or Sukrara's events in the picked calendar.
- Only the wearer's phone writes; followers never do. A phone that stops being the wearer removes what it wrote and
  turns the setting off (the group is hidden on a follower).

**States and exits**: *Off* → switch on → Android's permission dialog → *Allow* (on, the event appears) / *Don't
allow* (off, says why; switch again to ask again) / refused for good (switch opens Android's app page; back → switch
again). *On* → switch off (events removed). *Calendar* → dialog with Sukrara's own calendar and every writable one →
pick (events move) / *Cancel*. *Show in Google Calendar* → Google Calendar (back returns). Permission revoked in
Android → off on return. TalkBack reads the switch with its summary and the calendar dialog as radio buttons.

## Share with followers (wearer)

The wearer's phone shares readings, alerts and the stream's roster with approved followers over Nostr relays
(contracts/protocol/PROTOCOL-v2.md; `apps/android/share`).

- **Identity**: one sharing identity (key I) per wearer, created on first use (setup's *Start fresh*: the person's name
  and picture first, a name is needed) or **restored** from the password manager or a recovery code (the `ncryptsec` /
  `nsec` of *Recovery code*). Kept encrypted with the Android Keystore (`sukrara.identity.owner`), never in backups or
  logs.
- **How you appear** (home's top bar: the picture, the name or *Add your name*, *N followers*; Settings › Sharing's
  first row): name (≤ 40 characters, trimmed, blank keeps the previous) and a generated picture (*Shuffle*;
  *Customize*: shape chips Cube / Drop / Dots / Rings / Letter, eight colour swatches named for TalkBack, *Turn the
  colours*). Pictures are drawn by THE avatar renderer (`core/designsystem` `AvatarDrawing` + `AvatarImage`, v1 of
  PROTOCOL-v2 §Profiles; a newer version or none draws the grey initial). *Save* sends the roster at once (and the
  backup); followers see it the next time their app connects. During *Another app is sharing as you* Save is refused
  with a note and the edit stays.
- **Backup** (home's *Not backed up* card, Settings › Sharing › Backup): *Save to password manager* (Credential Manager
  password `Sukrara · npub1…`, the system's sheet asks; saved / cancelled / failed shown under it), *Recovery code*
  (a password of ≥ 12 characters → the code in a secure dialog — no screenshots, hidden in Recents — *Copy and close*
  copies it marked sensitive), *Follower list on relays* (when the encrypted owner backup last went out). The phone
  counts as backed up once the password manager took it or a code was copied (a restore counts too); until then home
  shows *Not backed up* (dismissable, back after a while). Last, *Replace this identity* (see below).
- **Replace this identity** (Backup's last row; `backup/ReplaceIdentity.kt`): a working identity replaced without
  reinstalling. The page first says what followers lose, by name (*Your glucose stops reaching Dad and Marta right
  away.*, *Each one needs a new invite from you to follow you again. Until then their Sukrara warns that no data
  arrives.*, *Requests waiting for you and unused invites stop working.*; *No one follows this identity now.* when
  so), and, while the identity exists nowhere else, *This identity isn't backed up: once replaced, it's gone for good.*
  with *Back up first* (back to Backup). Then two ways: *Restore another identity* (the setup restore form: the same
  gate — this phone's own sensor live —, the password manager or a pasted code; *Replace with this identity*) or
  *Start fresh* (a new identity with how the wearer appears now). Each is confirmed in a dialog that names the followers
  again and repeats the backup warning; its button (*Replace* / *Start fresh*) is armed only by *I understand they must
  be invited again*. The engine replaces only when asked (`OwnerEngine.createIdentity` / `restore` with
  `replaceExisting`), never over an unreadable identity, and a code for the identity already in use changes nothing
  (*That code is for the identity this phone already shares as.*). The old identity stops publishing at once; followers
  aren't told (they see no data until they join again). Leaving the page doesn't stop a replace that started. Done:
  *Started fresh. No one follows this phone yet: invite your followers again.* with *Invite someone* (the share screen)
  and *Done*, or *Replaced. N followers of the restored identity follow this phone now.* with *Done*. Setup's *Start
  fresh* confirmation says the old identity can be restored later there. Tests: `OwnerEngineTest`,
  `ReplaceIdentityViewModelTest`, the `replace-*` gallery scenes.
- **Restore** (switching phones): *Restore from password manager* (only once the restore gate is open: the secret is
  never fetched before; an `ncryptsec` fills the code field and asks for its password; a saved password that isn't
  an `nsec` / `ncryptsec` is never used, it says so) or the pasted code → the newest
  owner backup on the relays →
  followers, invites, writers and profile come back; this phone gets a new device key and a new epoch, every follower
  gets the new read key before the new roster, so existing followers (Mac included) keep following without doing
  anything.
- **Invite** → single-use link (`https://sukrara.mauri.app/join#…`, 24 h, a label for who it's for); the first phone
  to use it claims it, later ones get *invite used* and the request shows how many others tried.
- **Approve / Deny** a request; **Remove** a follower → *revoked* notice + new epoch, so it can't read anything new.
  Writers (followers allowed to add treatments) are named in the roster. The **approve dialog** shows over whatever
  screen is open (not over setup or sensor pairing): the follower's picture and name (control / direction characters
  removed), *Your invite* with the wearer's label and a *Name matches* / *Different name* hint, *Sent 10:02 · asked
  10:05*, the 4-digit code to compare (read digit by digit by TalkBack), *Later* / *Deny* / *Allow*. Allow is armed
  600 ms after the dialog appears and works once per tap. When other phones also tried the same link the wearer types
  the code shown on the follower's phone instead (the engine refuses Allow without it). An Allow that didn't happen
  says why in a dialog over the app (*Couldn't give access*: the invite expired or was used — send a new one; or
  sharing is paused while another app shares as you).
- **Readings**: live event per new reading (< 10 min old), hour chunks of the last 25 h (current hour at most every
  5 min), all of the last 24 h again on start, approval and rotation. **Alerts** go out at once.
- **How readings travel** (Settings › Sharing, a group with *Over the internet*, *Upload interval* and *Over Bluetooth
  nearby*; `RelayPolicy`): **Over the internet** (globe icon, on by default, schema `sharing.nostr`; *Encrypted,
  through Nostr relays*). Off: the relays close and nothing more is published to them (readings, alerts, roster,
  treatments, settings, invites); Bluetooth followers still get every event; its summary says *Off. Followers who only
  use the internet see no new readings.* Control events wait (`pendingControl`), and turning it on again connects and
  sends what the relays missed: the waiting control events, the last 24 h of chunks, the treatment log and settings,
  roster / backup when due (not again over Bluetooth). **Upload interval** (schema `sharing.nostrInterval`, the one
  number input: *Every reading* (default) / 5 / 15 / 30 min / 1 h or 1–240 min; disabled with *Sharing over the
  internet is off* while it's off): with an interval no live events go to the relays and the current hour's chunk goes
  out at most every N min (earlier hours at once); alerts still go at once; Bluetooth followers stay live. *Share with
  someone* while internet sharing is off says an invite link and its request travel over the internet, offers *Turn on
  sharing over the internet* (or Pair nearby) and doesn't create a link. The interval is in the settings export; the
  switch isn't (who receives the data).
- **Access request notification** (channel *Access requests*, Sukrara's info group): *Ana wants to see your glucose ·
  Asked with your invite for Ana · 14:02* with **Deny** and **Allow** (Allow only on an unlocked phone); a tap opens
  the app. When another phone also tried the same invite there are no actions: open the app and check. The
  notification goes as soon as the request is answered anywhere (notification or app) or sharing pauses (another app
  sharing as you). An Allow that didn't happen (invite expired, request no longer waiting, sharing paused) is replaced
  by *Couldn't give access* (open Sukrara to send a new invite). Sharing runs only while the phone is the wearer.
- **Names someone else chose** (a follower's, the wearer's) are trimmed when typed, when received and when shown
  (control and direction-changing characters dropped too; `Profile.shownName` on both apps,
  `contracts/protocol/identity/profile-v1.json` `received` / `shown`); one that is empty reads *Someone* (*Alguien*) in
  the access request (notification, dialog, followers list) and *the person who invited you* / *the wearer* where the
  sentence names the wearer, never "?" or nothing.
- **Treatments** (PROTOCOL-v2 §Treatments): every change of the phone's treatment log (add, edit, delete, undo) goes
  to followers at once, signed by this phone's device key; the whole log of the last 30 days (deleted entries too)
  again on start, on a new epoch (restore, removing a follower), when a follower is approved, and weekly. **Can add
  entries** (writer scope) for an active follower: the roster names it; its entries are merged into the log only for
  a new entry or one it created (never the wearer's or another writer's), then echoed to every follower.
- **Mirrored settings** (PROTOCOL-v2 §Settings): exactly the schema's `mirror` settings (ranges, graph, per-alert
  on / threshold / repeat except no data, the three quiet rules) go to followers 3 s after a change, only when one of
  them changed (sounds, notification actions, Mute all and the alerts main switch never leave the phone); the same
  version again on start, a new epoch and weekly. Nothing is sent until the settings layer provides them.
- **Relays**: `wss://nos.lol`, `wss://relay.primal.net`, `wss://nostr.mom`, `wss://relay.snort.social`; reconnect
  2 s doubling to 5 min (random 50–100 %), at once when the network comes back; per-relay status, never an error
  thrown at the person. Roster and backup are refreshed every 6 days. A relay that sends a message over 1 MiB, or
  more messages than the phone can handle (4096 waiting), loses its socket (status *message too big* / *too many
  messages*) and is retried with the same backoff (Android; the Mac's URLSession caps messages at 1 MiB). On both
  apps an event a relay sends for a subscription that isn't open, or that doesn't match it, is dropped unread; a relay
  that sends more than 2000 events at once or 20 a second after that loses its socket (*too many events*) the same
  way; a follower remembers at most 5000 seen events (the least recently seen forgotten first). Relay lists from a
  roster, key delivery, backup, invite or nearby pairing keep at most 8 `wss://` relays with a public host (no
  `localhost`, `.local` / `.lan` names or private addresses; `contracts/protocol/relays/relay-lists.json`); a debug
  build's local test relay comes from the `RELAYS` debug command, never from a list. Readings (live or in an hour
  chunk) more than 5 min in the future are refused.
- **Screens** (`feature/sharing` `share/`, `followers/`, `conflict/`; Settings › Sharing = `SharingScreen`
  under the settings page's title and Back (system / predictive back too): no identity → *Set up sharing* (setup's
  followers step alone; done or Back → Settings › Sharing, Back also leaves *Restore your followers* on home's
  Finish setup card), sharing → Followers, conflict → the conflict screen): *Share with someone* (a label only
  the wearer sees, *Create invite* → the QR springs in with a confirm haptic, the link as selectable text, *Send link* /
  *Copy*, "works once, until <date>", *They'll see*, *Invite someone else*); *Settings › Sharing* (your row → How you
  appear, *Backup*, *Share with someone*, *Waiting for you* requests → the approve dialog, followers with their
  pictures, since <date> and *Can add entries* / *Sees only* → dialog with the picture, a *Can add entries* switch — a
  refused change snaps back and says why — and *Remove* confirmed "They stop seeing your data. To follow again they need
  a new invite."; removed followers folded under an *N Removed* pill → their group with the date, *See less*);
  *Another app is sharing as you* screen (what
  happened, whether followers still receive readings, *I turned it off. Retry*,
  *Close*; it blocks only the sharing area — readings and alerts keep running).
- **Another app sharing as you**: a newer roster / backup signed by this identity from another device (the
  old phone still running after a restore) blocks roster, backup, rotation and follower changes instead of
  republishing back and forth; readings and alerts continue. The retry first reads the other app's newest roster and
  backup from the relays; when the other app moved its followers to an epoch this phone doesn't hold, it rotates above

- **Wearer** (Android, Settings › Sharing): *Over Bluetooth nearby* in the *How readings travel* group (Bluetooth icon,
  on by default; schema `sharing.bleFollowers`). Its line says what holds: *2 devices connected nearby*, *On · no

  sharing over the internet is off too). The page looks again on resume and after the permission answer.
- While the switch is on, *Nearby devices* lists the follower devices connected now (*Connected*, oldest link first;
  *Bea · phone* / *Sam · Mac*, their picture), then the followers paired with *Pair nearby* that aren't connected
  (*Disconnected*, by name), like Android's Connected devices (`NearbyDevices.rows`, `NearbyDevicesTest`). Which
  followers paired nearby is kept on this phone only (`OwnerState.nearbyPaired`, never in the owner backup) and cleared
  by Remove. The follower's dialog says *Connected* / *Disconnected* for such a device. A connection is named only by the identity proof in its `HELLO`
  (poc/spec/ogs-ble.md B§5/B§6): the follower signs a kind-21080 event with content `H("cgm2:ble-id:"+sid+":"+nonce+":"+dev)`
  with its follower key, and the wearer's phone checks it against each active follower and device type
  (`HelloProof.identify`; vector `ble.hello_id`). An unnamed link is not listed (no made-up name); a replayed `HELLO`
  names nothing. A row opens the follower's dialog; *Remove* there also drops that follower's Bluetooth links
  (`OwnerEngine.forget`), and a device that connects again is neither named nor trusted.
- The wearer's phone (`AndroidNearbyServer`, protocol in `NearbyServerCore`) advertises the Sukrara service connectable
  with **no name** (no scan response), opens a new GATT server after Bluetooth comes back, and stops with a visible state
  when the permission is revoked (never a crash). It pushes every event it publishes except the owner backup and the
  invite wrap, which stay on the relays; live and alert pushes go ahead of a long backlog. Events a follower device writes
  are trusted only by their signatures: gift wraps to the identity are verified before they count as seen (requests,
  rekeys, history requests), a writer's entry goes through the writer rules and is forwarded to the other devices.
- **Follower** (Android, `AndroidNearbyCentral` + `NearbyLink`; Mac `NearbyCentral`): while following, Bluetooth runs
  beside the relays; copies are one reading (deduplicated by event id, one reading source). The Following page says
  *Bluetooth: connected to their phone* only once the phone's `INFO` proved it's this wearer's; otherwise *looking for
  their phone*, *Bluetooth is off* (*Turn on Bluetooth*), *Needs the Nearby devices permission* (*Allow Nearby devices*:
  scan + connect, `neverForLocation`) or *not connected* (monitoring stopped).
- `HELLO` carries a fresh random nonce; the phone's `INFO` answers `m = H("cgm2:ble:"+sid+":"+nonce)`. A follower writes
  nothing to a phone (requests, rekeys, history requests, treatments) until its `INFO` proves our stream (`m` over one
  of its last 4 nonces; once the follower knows the stream id a pairing `i` never proves it). Another wearer's phone is
  avoided for 10 minutes (that phone only, by its current Bluetooth address / Mac peripheral id: never the one connected
  since; a phone that rotates its address sooner can be tried again) and the scan goes on; on
  the Mac, a phone with nothing to pair with (not following yet) is left for a minute. A pending request: receive only.
  After a new key the follower sends `HELLO` again with its proof. Entries still pending go to the phone as soon as it
  proved it's the wearer's; one counts as delivered on a relay's OK or the wearer's echo (a bare write response doesn't).
- Reliability (Android follower): scan by service UUID (starts spaced: at most 4 per 30 s; restarted every 25 min), one
  connection at a time with a 25 s connect timeout (that phone skipped 60 s), MTU 517, a 10 s timeout on every GATT
  step, `HELLO` again after 3 min without data, link dropped after 5 min, rescan after 3 s doubling to 1 min while
  connections fail, a new `BluetoothGatt` per connection.
- Frame sizes: every frame fits one ATT write / notification at the link's MTU (Android central: the negotiated MTU;
  Mac: CoreBluetooth's write-without-response length + 3), so a Mac at the default MTU 23 needs no long write; the
  wearer's phone still accepts a long (Prepare/Execute) write as one frame (`contracts/protocol/ble/default-mtu.json`).
- Limits: reassembly drops a message idle for 30 s or over 256 KiB, at most 16 open at once (Android and Mac). The
  wearer's phone takes at most 8 follower devices at once (a connection counts only once it writes to the service), checks
  each write before answering (a malformed frame, a long write out of offset order or over 512 bytes, or more than
  256 KiB a minute → error and disconnect;
  more than 30 messages a minute → dropped), sends one backlog per device per 5 s (a sooner `HELLO` is answered when due),
  and never re-sends an expired event from its backlog.
- **Pair nearby** (Android wearer, Settings › Sharing › How readings travel, shown while Bluetooth can serve): a switch that opens
  a single-use invite for 10 minutes (`OwnerEngine.openNearbyPairing`; no link and no invite wrap on the relays, the
  backup records the token). While open the phone advertises even with no follower and its `INFO` carries `i`, `relays`
  and `pair` (token, expiry, the wearer's profile and `c`, the commitment to a fresh pairing nonce; PROTOCOL-v2
  §Nearby pairing); the summary counts down (*Open · closes in 9:41*). It closes at the first request with it (one that
  carries the follower's nonce `n`; one without claims nothing), after 10 minutes, with the switch (*Stop*), when *Over
  Bluetooth nearby* is turned off or when sharing stops or the app restarts; each drops the unclaimed invite (a restore
  keeps an invite nearby). The request that claims it gets the wearer's nonce at once (`pair`, over the relays and
  Bluetooth), so both phones show the same **6-digit pairing code** (`021 472`; a numeric comparison as in Bluetooth LE
  Secure Connections pairing: a phone in the middle can't make the two codes match). The approve dialog says anyone in
  Bluetooth range can ask and shows the code big (*Allow only if their phone shows this same code*; TalkBack reads it
  digit by digit); its notification has no Allow (*Open Sukrara and check that their phone shows the same code*).
  Vectors: `contracts/protocol/ble/nearby-pairing.json` (`NearbyPairingVectorsTest`, Mac `NearbyPairingVectorsTests`).
- **Find phones nearby** (Android follower, setup's join step, under the link field): looks over Bluetooth while the list
  shows (`FollowerEngine.findNearby`): each phone found is connected in turn, sent a `HELLO` without a proof, its `INFO`

  can't stay listed); one that stops offering leaves the list at its next read, one that doesn't answer 12 s after the

  checked to be https) gets a token; the token and the profile fields the sensor registration repeats are kept only in
  the Keystore-encrypted secure store (`libreview`); the password never leaves the dialog's memory and that request.

  uploading) or *Cancel*: Sukrara never takes over by itself. "The same account" as the last sign-in (what LibreView

  side has no AccountId. If the account isn't the one Sukrara starts and takes over sensors with (Settings › Sensor ›
  LibreView account: its receiver ID ≠ `ReceiverIds.fromAccountId(AccountId)`), nothing is saved yet and the dialog
  asks *Not your sensor's LibreView account* (glucose and entries would go to this account; the clinic won't see them
  in the other one) with *Upload to this account* or *Cancel* (that sign-in is forgotten; a take-over already made stays,
  LibreView gives no AccountId before it). While signed in to such an account the *Account* group says *Not the account
  in Settings › Sensor* (warning icon). No receiver ID on the phone (Settings › Sensor not signed in): no question.
- *Upload to LibreView* (enabled once signed in; turning it off always works, also after signing out or on a follower
  phone, which sees "Only the phone that wears the sensor uploads"), *Status* ("Last upload 4 min ago · 3 items", "On ·
  nothing sent yet", "<reason> Trying again soon.", "LibreView asks you to sign in again (another app may have taken
  over the account)" — tapping it opens the sign-in).
- *What to send*: *Glucose* ("From a Libre 3 connected to this phone, since <date time>") and *Insulin, carbs and
  notes* (entries made or changed from now on; edits and deletes correct what LibreView has); both on, disabled while
  the upload is off. *Upload every* (`apps.libreviewInterval`, `NumberDialog`: 5 / 15 / 60 min or a custom 1–240 min,
  default 5; disabled while the upload is off): the upload's cycle, and new glucose wakes it only once that long has
  passed since the last upload; entry and setting changes still go at once. Each part goes up only from the moment it (or the upload) was switched on: the person's earlier
  LibreView data (from LibreLink or another app) is never doubled. *Sign out* (confirmed) forgets the token; LibreView
  keeps what it has.
- What goes up: exactly what the FreeStyle Libre 3 app sends (contracts/treatments/SPEC.md §3, byte for byte). Entries:
  one record per version (`recordNumber` from the entry id and version); an edit sends a retraction of the version
  LibreView has plus the new one, a delete only the retraction, one deleted before it went up nothing. What LibreView
  got is remembered for a year (typed store `libreview-upload`), so later edits still retract it; entries made on
  another device wait 2 min. Glucose: only readings Sukrara takes from a Libre 3 itself (the sensor link tags each
  batch with its serial; Juggluco's readings never go up), record number = the serial's base | life count; the newest
  reading as current (< 5 min old), the 5-minute history, `sensorstart` once per sensor after `PUT /api/nisperson`
  registered it (skipped if sign-in didn't return the profile, so it can't blank it). History LibreView hasn't got yet
  is also kept in the typed store `libreview-unsent` (one sensor, at most 3 days, only what would go up; rewritten
  when it changes and after each upload), so a restart or a process death never loses it: the next cycle sends it.
- When: after each new Libre 3 reading, entry change or setting change, and every 5 minutes. At most 100 entries and
  288 readings per upload; a bigger catch-up continues at once. Network or server trouble retries after 30 s doubling
  to 15 min (±20 %); a token LibreView no longer accepts (401 / 403 / `wrongDeviceInToken`) stops until the person
  signs in again (unlike the contract's re-auth: the password isn't kept); entries LibreView refuses (`status` ≠ 0)
  wait until an entry or a setting changes (glucose goes on without them), refused glucose retries with the backoff,
  a refused sensor registration holds that sensor's glucose until the next sign-in; the reason is in the status. An
  entry whose kind changed is retracted under the kind LibreView has. Redirects are never followed.

- Home's treatment list (wearer, while the upload is signed in and on with *Insulin, carbs and notes*): a small grey
  cloud after each entry the upload covers, by the uploader's own rules (`LibreViewQueue.mark`): cloud with a tick =

  older than 30 days, get none. Deleting an entry LibreView has while the upload runs (not stopped) says "Treatment deleted. It will be removed from LibreView

## Nightscout upload (docs/25)

- Settings → Share with apps → *Nightscout* → *Nightscout upload* (wearer only; Android only, the Mac doesn't upload;
  `:integrations` `NightscoutUploader`, `NightscoutPage`; `NightscoutUploaderTest` against MockWebServer). **Off by

- *Site* opens a dialog: address, *Access token* or *API secret*, the token / secret field (empty keeps the saved one of

  site refused this token or secret", "Couldn't connect. <reason>") and *Save*. The token or secret lives only in the
  Keystore-encrypted secure store (`nightscout`), never in settings, logs or backups, and is never shown again.
- Addresses: `https://` with a certificate from a public authority (user-installed / private CAs aren't trusted, so a
  self-signed home server fails *Test* with a TLS error); `http://` only to this phone (`localhost`, `127.0.0.1`),
  the only cleartext the network security config allows. Any other `http://`, home network included, shows "Use
  https://: Sukrara sends your data only encrypted" (issue filed: a home-network `http://` site needs a network-config
  and local-network-permission decision). A redirect is never followed (it could carry the secret elsewhere): it shows as
  a retry with "That address doesn't work. Check it, including https://.".
- *Upload to my Nightscout* (enabled once a site is saved; a follower phone sees "Only the phone that wears the sensor
  uploads"), *Status* ("Last upload 3 min ago · 12 items", "On · nothing sent yet", "<reason> Trying again soon.",
  "The site refused the token or secret: change it" — tapping it opens the site dialog —, "The saved token can't be read:
  enter it again" when the secure store can't decrypt it any more — tapping it opens the site dialog, which then needs
  the token or secret typed again — (a Keystore that is only busy is never shown as "Set up the site first": it reads as
  "<reason> Trying again soon." and is tried again with the backoff), "Paused: another app (<device>) uploads to this
  site" with *Upload anyway*).
- *What to send*: *Glucose readings* (every reading, the last 30 days included), *Insulin, carbs and notes* (edits and
  deletions too), *Sensor changes*, *Phone battery*; all on, disabled while the upload is off. *Advanced*: *API* (v1
  recommended; v3 only with an access token, a secret keeps v1) and *Remove this site* (confirmation; forgets the site,
  the credential and what was sent; the site keeps its data).
- What goes up: only readings this phone took from its own sensor (Libre 3 or Juggluco; never a followed person's or
  test data): `sgv`, integer `date`, `dateString` (local on v1, UTC on v3), `utcOffset`, `direction` or `NONE`, 5-minute
  `delta`, `noise` 1, `app` `Sukrara`, `device` this install's own name `Sukrara-<6 hex>` (chosen when the site is set,
  so another app or another install is never mistaken for this one), with the family after it for an untested sensor
  (`Sukrara-<6 hex> · Dexcom G7 (untested)`, also G6, Accu-Chek SmartGuide, Aidex X; `NsDocs.entryDevice`, `NsDocsTest`)
  so downstream apps can tell those readings apart; the conflict pause counts both names as this uploader;
  Nightscout's v5 identifier; rapid insulin as
  `Correction Bolus`, carbs as `Carb Correction`, notes as `Note`, long-acting as a `Note` with the dose only in `notes`;
  a new sensor as `Sensor Change` + `Sensor Start`, the notes naming the real family (*Libre 3 <serial>*, *Dexcom G7
  (untested) <serial>*); the battery as devicestatus when it changes or every 30 min.
- When: a cycle at start, after each new reading, entry change or setting change, and every 5 minutes (never in the
  reading path). v1 sends 500 readings per request; v3 one per request, at most 240 per cycle.
- Catch-up and duplicates: what the site lacks is planned from the phone's store every cycle; on the first run, after
  failures and every 6 h the phone compares with the site's readings: minutes the site lost are sent again, minutes
  within 2 min of another uploader's reading count as covered (a backfill never doubles someone else's data), and
  another device's readings in the last 30 min **pause everything** (readings, entries, sensor, battery) until *Upload
  anyway*. Failures retry after 30 s doubling to 15 min (±20 %, or the site's Retry-After); 401 / 403 stops until the
  token is changed.
- Entries another app already uploaded (AndroidAPS's pump bolus, xDrip+, a pump's app, the careportal) are never
  doubled: Nightscout merges a treatment only on its `identifier` (or, without one, on time + eventType), so the site
  would keep both and count the insulin / carbs twice for everyone reading it, loops included. Before an entry goes up
  the first time (and after an edit of a linked one) the site's treatments within ±2 min are read (`/api/v1/treatments.json`
  by `created_at`, both APIs): one from another uploader with the same amount in the field Nightscout counts — rapid
  insulin ↔ `insulin` (±0.01 U), carbs ↔ `carbs` (±0.5 g), whatever its eventType (`Meal Bolus`, `SMB`, `<none>`…); a
  note or long-acting dose ↔ a `Note` with the same text — **links** the entry instead of sending it (`NsState.linked`,
  `NsDocs.sameTreatment`; `NsDocsTest`, `NightscoutUploaderTest`). Never one of Sukrara's own documents, a soft-deleted
  one, or the same field of one already linked (a `Meal Bolus` covers one insulin and one carbs entry). The other app's
  document stays as it is: deleting a linked entry sends nothing; an edit that no longer matches goes up as Sukrara's
  own. A site that refuses that lookup drops the entry like a refused document (never sent blind). Upload only: nothing
  is read back except those reading dates and treatments.

**States and exits**: *Not set up* → *Site* → dialog (Cancel keeps nothing). *Off* (default) → main switch. *On ·

## Languages

- English (default) and Spanish (Spain) everywhere, settings pages included (every page, alert page, dialog, search and the alerts page's "lately" list); the phone's language picks one, or Settings → Apps → Sukrara → Language (per-app language, Android 13+).
- Settings search finds rows in the shown language, accents optional ("insulina rapida" finds "Tras insulina rápida"): the index is built from the pages as they are drawn.
- Settings words follow Android's own Spanish where a stock setting exists: Ajustes, Activado / Desactivado, Pantalla de bloqueo, Barra de estado, No molestar, Restablecer, Buscar en Ajustes.
- Text lives in `res/values/strings.xml` and `res/values-es/strings.xml`; a unit test fails when a key or a format argument is missing in a translation.
- Numbers, dates and times follow the chosen language (decimal comma in Spanish, localized day and month names); units stay as they are (mg/dL, `4.5u`, `30g`).
- Every clock time follows the device's 12/24-hour setting, not the language's default (Android: Settings → System → Date & time → *Use 24-hour format*, read live; Mac: System Settings → Date & Time → *24-hour time*): `21:08` / `9:08 PM` (es `9:08 p. m.`). That covers the card (`Ready at`, *No reading since*, a late reading's time), the graph's hour labels, widgets, lock screen widget, the live notification, sensor reminders and countdowns, the calendar event's description, treatments (list, dialog, followers' entries), alerts list, access requests, settings summaries (reminder times, sensor end, LibreView), the Mac menubar tooltip, dropdown, report and treatments. One helper per platform (Android `graph/Clock.kt`, Mac `Clock` in SukraraGraph.swift), and it is the only place the setting is read (Android: `Clock.hour24Source`, installed by the app; Mac: `Clock.hour24`, formatting with the bare locale so the cycle asked for is the one shown): no surface passes a 24-hour flag of its own (2026-10-21: the value widget showed `08:59` beside the chart cards' `8:59 AM`). Shared cases in `poc/spikes/graph-fixtures/clock`; every surface is rendered in 12- and 24-hour time by the cross-surface tests (`clock12` / `clock24`: every time any surface formats or draws must be in that cycle); `tools/check` `clock` rejects an own flag or time formatter. Switching the setting (or the time zone) while Sukrara runs redraws everything at once: Android's monitor receiver takes Settings' `ACTION_TIME_CHANGED` / `ACTION_TIMEZONE_CHANGED` and re-renders the notification and the widgets (`MonitorModule.onClockChanged`: a redraw tick), and `Clock.signature()` is in every redraw key (the notification's card, the widgets' `DrawKey` with the language too; `WidgetUpdaterTest`, #148); the Mac redraws the menubar and dropdown and re-publishes to the widgets on the current locale's change and the time zone's (`Clock.observeChanges`, `-graphselftest`). The log and debug output keep 24 h.
- Spanish terms: hidratos (carbs), insulina rápida / lenta, tiempo en rango, seguidores; alert headlines like "Glucosa baja · come más".
- Changing the language (the phone's, or Sukrara's own per-app language) renames the notification channels and groups (the person's channel settings stay) and redraws the glucose notification and home's *Finish setup* step at once; the next alert is worded in the new language (Android: `App.onConfigurationChanged` and the monitor's `ACTION_LOCALE_CHANGED` receiver → `MonitorModule.onLocaleChanged`). The widgets redraw at once too (the language is in their redraw key).
- Alert rules and their timeline scenarios stay in English: the engine reports the kind and what was logged, and the notification and the settings pages word them. The log stays in English.

## Juggluco on this network (Mac)

- Settings › General › *Source*: the switch *Juggluco on this network* (schema `source.jugglucoLan`, off by default), an
  *Address* field (`source.jugglucoAddress`; blank = *Find it automatically*; Return or closing the pane saves it) and
  an *API secret* field (`source.jugglucoSecret`, kept in the Keychain, never in the defaults). The footer says where
  to turn Juggluco's server on (Settings › Web server, *Local only* off) and that it is plain http on the home network:
  readings travel unencrypted between the phone and the Mac. Dev, fixture and snapshot runs have no source (the
  switch is dimmed: *Not available in this run*); `ServicesPlan.lan`.
- `SukraraShare/Lan`: with no address it sweeps the Mac's up, non-loopback IPv4 networks of at most /22 in the home
  ranges (10/8, 172.16/12, 192.168/16; never 169.254 or a public network, never the Mac itself), 64 probes at a time,
  for `/api/v1/status.json` saying `juggluco` on :17580. The probes carry no secret; a set secret goes only to hosts
  that refused for want of one. A typed address must be such an IPv4 address (with an optional `:port`); anything
  else (a name, a public address) is refused and never contacted. Then the history
  (`/api/v1/entries.json?interval=0`, `count` = the minutes since the newest stored reading, 180 to 1600; rate = `delta` / 5, none when `direction` is
  empty) and the live stream (`/x/live`, `glucose` events), header `api-secret: sha1hex(secret)`. A value that isn't a
  measurement (0, negative, above 1000, NaN) or from the future is dropped; below 40 / above 400 becomes LO / HI;
  entries written by Sukrara are refused (that server isn't Juggluco). Readings go into their own 31-day store
  (`juggluco/readings.csv`), apart from a followed wearer's. Reconnects with backoff 5 s → 60 s; a sweep that found
  nothing runs again every minute (a sweep that found only a Juggluco asking for another secret says so); the found address is swept for again after three failed connections; a wake or the network coming back tries again at once. macOS asks
  once for Local Network access (`NSLocalNetworkUsageDescription`, in Spanish from `Bundle/es.lproj/InfoPlist.strings`
  like the Bluetooth one; `LocalizationTests`). A refusal is its own state when macOS says so (the failed connection's
  path is unsatisfied as *local network denied*; `JugglucoApi.isLocalNetworkDenied`): *macOS doesn't let Sukrara use
  the local network. Turn Sukrara on in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Local Network.* with **Open System
  Settings…** (that pane) and *Try Again*; tried again every minute and when Sukrara becomes active. macOS doesn't
  always say so, so *Not found* keeps the hint and the same button.
- Sensor life: after the history Sukrara reads `/sgv.json?count=1&sensor=Y` (as Android's Juggluco source):
  `sensor_status` "dd-mm-yy HH:MM (…)" is the running sensor's start in the phone's local time (taken as the Mac's:
  same home), with the Libre 3 / 3 Plus wear (15 days) and warm-up (60 min), since Juggluco publishes neither. It is
  read again when a live event's `sensorId` changes (a new sensor). It feeds the card's sensor life (ring, countdown,
  warm-up, sensor ended) and the alert rules' sensor window like a followed wearer's sensor; an older Juggluco
  without it, or a failed request, keeps the last one known and the readings go on.
- The readings feed the card, the rings, the widgets, the report and the alerts exactly as a followed wearer's do
  (this Mac's own alert rules, no mirroring). Following someone always wins: while following, the source is paused
  and never mixed with the wearer's readings (two people's values must never be interleaved).
- Monitoring starts at the first successful connection; from then on a lost Juggluco keeps monitoring and the no-data
  alert rings if it doesn't come back. Before that, the panel says why nothing is shown (*looking for Juggluco on this
  network*, *Juggluco wasn't found on this network*, *can't reach Juggluco at …*, *Juggluco refused the API secret*,
  *… isn't an address on your home network*, *macOS doesn't let Sukrara use the local network*, *Waiting for the
  first reading from Juggluco*).
- Tests: `JugglucoLanTests` (formats, address ranges, the sweep, the secret only to the host that asked, a dropped
  stream, the sensor's life and a new sensor, a refused local network) and `AppModelSourceTests` over a fake network (`FakeLanHTTP`); nothing touches a real network.

**States and exits** (Settings › General › Source): *Off* → turn the switch on. *Looking…* → found or not found by
itself; switch off. *Not found* → *Try Again*, type the address, or switch off. *Reading Juggluco at …* → switch off.
*Can't reach Juggluco at …* (retrying) → comes back by itself, *Try Again*, fix the address, switch off; the no-data
alert covers a long gap. *Juggluco refused the API secret* → type the secret, *Try Again*, switch off. *Not a
home-network address* → fix or clear the address, switch off. *macOS doesn't let Sukrara use the local network* →
**Open System Settings…** (turn Sukrara on there; back in Sukrara it looks again at once), *Try Again*, switch off.
*Paused while following …* → stop following
(Settings › Following) or switch off. Every row is reachable with VoiceOver (the state line reads as one element).
